1The plan in one page

Every MMG event gets a budget that stays true from the first plan to the final close. For every line item it shows what we planned to spend, what we have promised to suppliers, what has been billed, and what has been paid. Buying anything goes through a request, an approval and a purchase order, so the budget updates itself instead of depending on someone remembering to update a spreadsheet.

Purchase orders are posted to QuickBooks. Bills and payments that Muthu records in QuickBooks flow back into the budget. Once that is working, the revenue side is added: registrations and sponsorship money against expenses, so each event shows its margin.

What changes for people

  • Staff raise spend requests inside EA-SYS, where they already log in, instead of ProcurementExpress.
  • Lina and Medhat approve from an Approvals page in EA-SYS that reminds and escalates on its own. Email only tells them something is waiting.
  • Muthu keeps recording bills and payments in QuickBooks, with one rule: a bill against a purchase order is entered from that order and carries the event code, so the module can read it back onto the right line item.
  • Event owners get one dashboard per event and one view across every event in the year.
  • A new budget starts from the previous edition, or a similar event, compared per attendee, instead of from a blank page.
  • ProcurementExpress is cancelled after the first clean month-end close on the new module.

The one rule behind everything: EA-SYS is where decisions are made and recorded. QuickBooks is where postings land. Anything changed directly in QuickBooks is shown as a difference to resolve, never quietly copied over.

2The four numbers on every line item

Each line item (say "AV and production" for one event) carries four figures. They are the whole point of the module.

Planned

What the approved budget says we will spend on this line item.

Committed

Purchase orders issued and not yet billed. The promise we have made to a supplier.

Actual

Bills received against those purchase orders. As a bill arrives, value moves from committed to actual.

Paid

Money that has actually left the bank, read back from QuickBooks.

Remaining = Planned minus open commitments minus Actual. This is why "remaining" is truthful from day one: a purchase order reduces it the moment it is approved, not weeks later when the bill turns up.

Forecast is where the line item is expected to finish: the plan, or more as soon as commitments and bills exceed it, and the owner can adjust it with a reason. When the forecast for the whole event is higher than planned plus contingency, the budget is flagged at risk on the dashboard and by email to the owner and Muthu, weeks before the invoices arrive.

  • Every figure is excluding VAT, with the VAT amount shown beside it. Finance signed this off on 3 September 2026.
  • A budget is kept in one currency of its choice (AED, USD, EUR, GBP or SAR). Exchange rates are fixed on each line item when it is written, so a rate change later never rewrites history.
  • Approval limits and the cross-event view are always in AED.
  • Contingency (10% by default) is its own line item and is not counted inside the planned total.
  • Bills that arrive for an event with no purchase order behind them are shown in a separate "unallocated" bucket, so nothing is hidden and finance can see the gap between the module and the QuickBooks profit and loss.

3How a budget lives

A budget is not a spreadsheet uploaded once. It is proposed, approved, revised, drawn on, frozen and closed, and each step leaves a trail. ProcurementExpress never had this half.

  1. Start from a template. One template per event type (conference, webinar, hybrid) with the usual line items already listed. Editing a template later never changes a live budget.
  2. Fill in the line items. Quantity, unit cost, currency, VAT code, dates, supplier if known. Totals, VAT and contingency recalculate as you type. Where a benchmark has been chosen (section 7), its figures sit beside each category for reference (from Phase 6).
  3. Submit for approval. Muthu checks it is complete (every category has line items or is marked not applicable; contingency, currency and expected attendance set). Then it is approved on the same limits as spending: Lina up to AED 1,000,000 of planned expense (contingency excluded), Medhat above.
  4. Active. Only an active budget accepts spend requests.
  5. Revise through versions. Changing planned amounts creates a new version that goes through approval. The old version stays active until then, so spending never stops during a revision. Committed and actual figures follow each line item into the new version. A compare view shows old against new, the difference, and who approved it. A revision whose change is within Lina's limit can be approved by Lina even when Medhat approved the original total.
  6. Move money between line items. An event owner may move up to 10% of a line item's approved amount per version, and never into a line item that carries their own pending request; larger moves are approved on the same limits as spending. Every move is recorded. Spending from contingency is a request against the contingency line item, approved on the same limits.
  7. Freeze before the event. On demand or on a set date (typically two weeks out) the planned amounts lock and money can no longer be moved between line items. Spending continues on the normal limits; only a request that exceeds its line item on a frozen budget goes to Medhat as an exception.
  8. Close out after the event. Once every bill is allocated, or written off by Muthu, the owner closes the budget. Any line item more than 10% or AED 5,000 off plan (whichever is larger) needs a written explanation. A close-out summary is produced, listing any written-off bills separately so that it reconciles to the QuickBooks figure for the event; Muthu signs it off, and the budget becomes read-only. Reopening needs an admin and a reason.

4Buying something

This is the daily flow, so its screens get the most polish. From request to paid bill:

  1. Raise the request. The requester picks the event and the budget line item. The form shows what is left on that line item, open commitments, past dealings with the supplier, and who will approve.
  2. Attach quotes. A new supplier can be proposed on the same form. Muthu approves the supplier (tax number, terms, currency) before any purchase order can be issued to them; an approved request waits for that if it has to.
  3. Budget check. Within budget: goes to Lina, or to Medhat above AED 1,000,000. Over budget: blocked and routed to Medhat as an exception. It is never silently allowed through.
  4. Approval. The approver decides on the Approvals page in EA-SYS. The email is a notification with a link, not where the decision is taken. Reminder after 24 hours; after 48 hours without a decision it passes to Medhat. A request Lina raises herself goes straight to Medhat. Medhat is the top of the chain, so a request waiting on him is reminded daily; there is no standby for him in the first release.
  5. Purchase order. Approval creates the purchase order and emails the PDF to the supplier straight away, using the same layout as our invoices and quotes. Posting to QuickBooks follows in the background and never holds the supplier up.
  6. Receiving. The requester marks the order partly or fully received; above AED 50,000 a second person confirms it.
  7. Bill and payment. Muthu records the bill in QuickBooks from the purchase order, and the payment against it. Within 24 hours the line item shows the new actual and paid figures. A bill that arrives with no order behind it goes to the unallocated bucket, where Muthu links it to an order or writes it off. A supplier credit note reduces the line item it relates to.
  8. Changes after approval. A changed amount is re-approved for the difference, by whoever the new total requires. A purchase order already posted is cancelled and re-issued rather than edited, so QuickBooks and EA-SYS never disagree about it.

Hard rules, with no way round them:

  • Nobody can approve their own request. A request raised by an approver goes to the level above them.
  • A delegate can never be the person who raised the request.
  • Limits are checked in AED at the moment of the decision.
  • Supplier bank details and tax numbers are treated like passport and bank details in the speaker reimbursement module: visible to finance and admins only, never quoted in the audit trail.

6Revenue and margin (Phase 5)

Controlling expenses tells you what an event cost, not whether it made money. The revenue side uses figures EA-SYS and the CRM already hold, so it is mostly reading, not typing.

  • Planned revenue: one line item per registration type (expected numbers times price, taken from the event's registration types), one per sponsorship package or exhibitor (from the CRM pipeline), plus grants and other income.
  • Actual revenue is calculated, never typed: paid registrations at the price actually paid (after promo codes and refunds), and won sponsorship deals at their value. Only "other" income is entered by hand.
  • Margin: revenue minus expense on the same excluding-VAT basis, shown as an amount and a percentage against the target set on the budget. Below target flags the event at risk, the same way an expense overrun does.
  • How each figure is computed (which price, refunds, group bookings, sponsor-paid places) is written down in the technical part, §6b.
  • Not included: posting revenue to QuickBooks (it is already invoiced through EA-SYS and the CRM), deferred revenue accounting, and sharing costs across events. Each of those would be a later decision.

7Benchmarking against past events

A new budget should not start from a blank page. When it is created, the organiser chooses what it is measured against, and that reference sits beside every figure until the budget closes.

  • Repeat event: pick the previous edition. For an event that has already run in EA-SYS that is its closed budget, line item by line item. For anything before October 2026 it is the archive entry for that event: spend by category from QuickBooks and ProcurementExpress, attendance and revenue from EventsAir. A previous edition that is still open can be used as it stands, marked as in progress.
  • First-time event: the system suggests up to three similar past events (same type, same brand, a comparable expected attendance) and the organiser picks one. Suggested, never chosen automatically: a wrong reference quietly picked by the system is worse than none.
  • What is compared: planned against planned while the budget is being written and, at close-out, actual against actual; for each category, and for each line item that came from the same template line item. Totals are also shown per attendee, because a 300-delegate meeting measured against a 900-delegate congress only makes sense per head. Revenue and margin join the comparison once the revenue phase has landed.
  • Where it shows: a benchmark column in the budget editor, one line on the approver's screen ("12% above the 2026 edition per attendee"), the close-out summary (this edition against its benchmark) and the cross-event report (year over year by brand).

Planned from day one, built last. The comparison screens are Phase 6, after revenue, because by then there will be closed EA-SYS budgets worth comparing against. What cannot wait is the data that makes budgets comparable, so Phase 1 captures four things from the first day: the expected attendance on every budget, categories from the shared list with fixed codes (never free text), a pointer to the previous edition where there is one, and one archive entry per past event (spend by category from QuickBooks and ProcurementExpress, attendance and revenue from EventsAir), loaded once the QuickBooks connection exists.

One honest limit: benchmarks against events from before October 2026 are by category only, and only where finance can supply the figures. Comparison by line item starts with the first budgets closed in EA-SYS. An event with nothing comparable simply shows no benchmark.

8QuickBooks

  • One company. Everything posts to the single MME QuickBooks file. There is no separate entity dimension.
  • Event code is the QuickBooks Class. Finance already tracks events this way, so event spend stays reportable inside QuickBooks. Every purchase order line item carries the event code, and the Class is created when the budget is approved, so it exists before the first order. In the first phase the event code becomes unique across MMG and cannot change once a budget or order uses it.
  • Multi-currency is switched on in QuickBooks and required. The module double-checks this when it connects.
  • We only create in QuickBooks, never edit. Approved orders are posted as purchase orders. A change means cancel and re-issue; cancelling closes the order in QuickBooks and releases whatever was not yet billed. New suppliers are created in QuickBooks the first time they get an order.
  • We read back every night, and on demand: purchase orders, bills, supplier credit notes and payments. A bill linked to our order updates actual and paid. A bill for an event with no order behind it goes to the unallocated bucket. A payment updates paid.
  • Changes made directly in QuickBooks (an order edited, closed or deleted there) are flagged as "diverged" and shown to Muthu and the admins to resolve: accept the QuickBooks version, or cancel and re-issue ours.
  • Connection health. The QuickBooks connection expires periodically. The module warns ahead of time and Muthu or an admin re-authorises in a couple of clicks. An Integration Monitor screen shows the last sync, failures, the retry queue and the unallocated count. Nothing fails silently.
  • Proof before we rely on it. In the first week a short trial against QuickBooks confirms the connection, the Class lookup, a purchase order with an event code, and reading a linked bill back.

9Screens and reports

Budget screens, used every day:

  • Budget dashboard per event: planned, committed, actual, paid, remaining, forecast, at-risk flag, unallocated bills, contingency left, open approvals, failed syncs.
  • Budget editor with running totals and the contingency panel (the benchmark column from Phase 6).
  • Version compare: old against new, line item by line item, with the approver.
  • Moves between line items and contingency draws, with the authority rules built in.
  • Freeze and close-out, with variance explanations and the close-out summary.
  • Event profit and loss (Phase 5) and the cross-event view: every event this year by brand with planned, forecast, actual and margin.
  • Benchmark picker when a budget is created, a benchmark column in the editor, and the approver's "against last edition" line (Phase 6).

Procurement screens: spend request form with a live budget side panel · Approvals page (requests, budgets, exceptions, contingency draws) · unallocated bills, where Muthu links a bill to an order or writes it off · purchase order list with sync and receiving status, linked bills and the PDF · supplier list with the proposed-supplier queue · mapping admin · Integration Monitor. English first; Arabic later, platform-wide.

Reports: planned versus committed versus actual versus paid by event, with unallocated bills shown separately · variance by category with the owner's explanations · forecast and at-risk list across events · contingency usage · close-out summary · cross-event comparison by year and brand · event profit and loss (Phase 5) · this edition against its benchmark, per category and per attendee, and year over year by brand (Phase 6) · supplier spend by event · open commitments · approval turnaround. All exportable to CSV and Excel.

Expectation to set with finance: until the later "invoice exception" phase, the module's actual figure for an event will sit below the QuickBooks profit and loss, because bills without a purchase order are counted in the unallocated bucket rather than on a line item. The gap is visible, not hidden.

11Leaving ProcurementExpress

  • Everything ProcurementExpress does today that we rely on (budgets, approval routing, purchase order PDFs to suppliers, the monthly finance reports) is in the first release. Muthu will list the exact reports pulled today so nothing is missed.
  • Suppliers are exported and matched to existing QuickBooks vendors by tax number, then by name. Anything ambiguous is resolved by hand on a screen.
  • Open purchase orders already exist in QuickBooks, so they are linked, not re-created. The historical export is archived, not imported.
  • Live event budgets are rebuilt with their current committed amounts, so "remaining" is right on day one.
  • ProcurementExpress's own QuickBooks connection is disconnected only after ours is verified.
  • Parallel run with a pilot group, then one clean month-end close, then the subscription is cancelled. From the first day of building that is around month five to six.

12EventsAir

  • Budgets attach only to events that live in EA-SYS. Today that means the upcoming events from October 2026 onward; everything earlier is in EventsAir and is treated as history.
  • EventsAir history is archived and indexed, never imported into the live system (decision of 2 September 2026). An early export of past events, registrations, revenue and attendance goes to the backup bucket, with a searchable index, so it does not depend on how long EventsAir access lasts. Past spend by category comes from QuickBooks and ProcurementExpress, not from EventsAir, which never held it.
  • When a budget is created, its event code is checked against that index. A code that clashes with a historical event is refused, so year-on-year comparison by code stays possible without importing anything.
  • How long EventsAir stays in use is Medhat's decision. Nothing in this module depends on a date.

  • A budget cannot close with unallocated bills. Every line item over the variance threshold carries an explanation. The close-out summary reconciles to the QuickBooks figure for the event, with written-off bills listed separately. Closed budgets are read-only.
  • The year view lists every event by brand with planned, forecast and actual, from the same figures the event dashboards show.
  • Benchmark foundations (Phase 1): a budget cannot be submitted without its expected attendance; every category is a fixed code; a cloned event points at its previous edition; the archive entry table exists and closed budgets write to it (past events are loaded in Phase 4).
  • A spend request with quotes can be raised against an active budget. Over-budget requests are blocked and routed. A proposed supplier is approved before an order is issued.
  • Nobody approves their own request. Limits are checked in AED. Inaction is reminded and escalated with notifications.
  • Approved requests become purchase orders and the PDF reaches the supplier on approval, even when QuickBooks is unreachable; the order posts to QuickBooks with the event code in the background.
  • Bills and payments recorded in QuickBooks update actual and paid within 24 hours. Non-order bills appear as unallocated and can be linked to an order or written off. Supplier credit notes reduce the line item. An order edited in QuickBooks is flagged.
  • A staff member without a permission cannot write any procurement data; the settle permission cannot approve spend; a request raised by an approver goes to the level above.
  • Every important action (submission, approval, amount change, override, sync fix, close-out) is in the audit trail with before, after and reason. Sync failures are visible and can be re-run by hand. Nothing fails silently.
  • Migration is complete with linked (not re-created) orders, and one finance month-end close has passed on real data.
  • Phase 5: planned revenue by registration type and sponsorship package; actual revenue computed from paid registrations and won deals with no manual entry; margin against target on the event and cross-event views.

The screen in EA-SYS where Lina and Medhat see everything waiting for them (spend requests, budgets, over-budget exceptions, contingency draws) and decide. Email only notifies with a link; the decision is always taken in EA-SYS.
Spend request
The form a staff member fills in to ask for money to be spent. It carries quotes and goes through approval.
Purchase order (PO)
The approved commitment to a supplier. It is what "committed" counts, and what gets posted to QuickBooks and emailed to the supplier.
Committed, open commitment
Purchase order value not yet billed. As bills arrive, value moves from committed to actual.
Actual
Bills received against our purchase orders, matched line item by line item.
Unallocated
A bill for an event that has no purchase order behind it. Counted separately so it is not lost and not hidden.
Excluding VAT (ex-VAT)
Amounts shown without the VAT on the line item, which is recorded beside them. UAE VAT is recoverable, so this is the basis finance asked for; foreign VAT that cannot be recovered is treated as cost.
Settle permission
Muthu's permission: everything that keeps the books true (suppliers, receiving, bills, write-offs, the QuickBooks connection, close-out sign-off) and nothing that approves spend.
Write-off
A bill for an event that will never be matched to a purchase order, accepted by Muthu as a cost and listed separately at close-out.
Class (QuickBooks)
The label QuickBooks uses to tag transactions to an event. Our event code is that label.
Read-back
The nightly and on-demand pull of purchase orders, bills and payments from QuickBooks into the budget.
Diverged
A purchase order that was changed directly in QuickBooks so it no longer matches ours. Shown for finance to resolve.
Contingency
A reserve line item, 10% of the plan by default and kept outside the planned total, drawn on through a spend request approved on the normal limits.
Freeze
The point, typically two weeks before the event, when the plan stops changing and only spending continues.
Close-out
The final step after the event: all bills allocated, variances explained, summary signed off, budget made read-only.
Parallel run
A period when the pilot group uses the new module while ProcurementExpress is still available, before it is cancelled.
Benchmark
The past event a new budget is measured against: the previous edition, or a similar event chosen by the organiser from the system's suggestions.
Cost per attendee
A line item or total divided by the number of attendees, so events of different sizes can be compared honestly.

§2Governing Principles

  1. The budget is the product. Every screen answers one question, "where does this event stand against its plan", and everything else (spend requests, purchase orders, the accounting connector, the reports) exists to keep that answer truthful. A feature that does not improve the answer is out of scope.
  2. EA-SYS is the system of record — workflow, approvals, budget logic, event metadata, audit history, procurement decisions. A change made directly in QuickBooks is a divergence to be surfaced, never silently absorbed.
  3. QuickBooks is a posting target, not the architectural centre. QBO-specific logic lives only in the provider adapter and mapping config.
  4. Inside this module, no table, screen, or business rule named after QuickBooks. (Core already exports invoices in a QuickBooks format; the principle is scoped to the module.) Generic names: Commitment.externalDocumentId, AccountMapping.providerName.
  5. Event code is the reconciliation key on every transactional object.
  6. Replaceable connector: AccountingProvider interface; QuickBooksProvider first.
  7. One money basis everywhere: ex-VAT, in the budget's reporting currency, 4 decimals stored (§7).
  8. Nothing fails silently. Every sync failure, divergence, or unallocated amount is visible on a screen and in the audit log.
  9. Supplier and payment data is classified. Tax registration numbers, bank details and payment records are financial data about third parties. They follow the platform’s reimbursement boundary: staff-only reads, redacted outside it, never quoted in audit diffs. The security-and-privacy posture document (the one handed to health-authority clients) is updated before the pilot.

§6Core Workflows

Budget: create for event (optionally from template) → auto-version → enter lines (FX snapshot per line) → totals + contingency computed → submit → approval per workflow rules → active. New version clones lines with their lineKey; commitments follow the key.

Spend request (the daily-use flow — polish this UI): requester picks event + active budget + line → side panel shows available budget on the category/line, current open commitments, suggested approval route, vendor history → submits with quotes (and a proposed supplier if new) → budget availability check (ex-VAT, reporting currency): within budget → Lina (Medhat above AED 1M); over budget → blocked and routed as an exception to Medhat, never silent → approval (ceiling checked in AED) → supplier not yet approved? awaiting_supplier until the settle grant approves it → Commitment created with lines → PO PDF generated and emailed to the supplier at once, carrying commitmentNo (reusing the invoice / quote PDF layout in src/lib/pdf/document-layout.ts: logo, bill-to boxes, totals, plus a small line table for qty, unit cost and tax code; the certificate pipeline is a text overlay on an uploaded background and does not fit), send recorded in audit → outbox → posted to QBO as Purchase Order in the background → externalDocumentId stored, status posted. Procurement never waits on accounting (rev. 3.5): a failed or held post is an Integration Monitor item, never a supplier without an order.

Supplier onboarding: a proposed supplier rides along with the request; the settle grant approves the supplier (TRN, terms, currency) from the proposed-supplier queue on the supplier master; an approved request whose supplier is not yet approved waits in awaiting_supplier and converts on approval. Adapter creates the vendor in QBO on first commitment; externalVendorId stored.

Approver notifications: email on assignment, reminder at 24h, delegate at 48h, next tier at 96h (§8.3). At launch Lina’s delegate and next tier are both Medhat, so her inaction passes to him at 48h. Without push, escalation becomes the normal path.

Receiving: the requester marks a commitment partially/fully received; above AED 50,000 (a setting) a second person, the settle grant or an approver, confirms it. fulfillmentStatus drives the open-commitment view alongside bill linkage.

Operating rule the model depends on (rev. 3.5): a bill against a PO is created from that PO in QBO (Copy to Bill), so it carries LinkedTxn, and every bill line carries the event Class. Phase 0 checks how bills are entered today; the pilot adopts the rule. Without it every bill lands in the unallocated bucket and committed never falls.

Read-back (v1, read-only): nightly + on-demand CDC poll pulls PurchaseOrders, Bills, VendorCredits and BillPayments changed since cursor. - Bill lines with LinkedTxn.TxnLineId → that CommitmentLine's lineKey (explicit). Header-only linkage → proportional by PO line value (proportional), flagged for finance override. Amounts that do not reconcile → mismatch, surfaced. - Bills and Purchases carrying the event dimension but no PO link → recorded as unallocated actuals for the event (bucket visible in v1 with a manual link-to-PO or write-off action for the settle grant; the matching-rules exception queue is post-v1, §12). - VendorCredits → negative actual on the lines of the bill they credit (proportional when the credit does not name lines). - BillPayments → PaymentRecord (gross); line paid = gross × (invoice ex-VAT ÷ invoice gross), allocated across the bill’s lines pro rata; a payment against a vendor with no bill yet is a deposit on the commitment and settles onto the bill when it arrives. - FX: actual converts at the bill’s own QBO exchange rate and paid at the payment’s; the difference to the line’s planned snapshot rate is shown per line as FX variance, never folded into the line (§7.7). - Bill voided in QBO → local void + committedOpen restored + event emitted. - PO edited/closed/deleted in QBO → Commitment diverged, surfaced in Integration Monitor for ADMIN/finance resolution (accept QBO / void-and-recreate). - CDC cursor older than the 30-day window → automatic full re-list by date range, logged.

Post-approval changes: amount change after approval → routed on the new total and approved for the delta, reason captured, audited (a series of deltas cannot lift a request past a ceiling in pieces); posted PO → close and recreate (create-only policy). Cancelling a posted Commitment closes the QBO PO (POStatus = Closed, the one update the policy allows) through the adapter, releases committedOpen for the unbilled remainder and keeps actual for what was billed; the week-1 spike confirms which operations QBO offers per entity (a PurchaseOrder is closed or deleted, never voided). Moving budget between lines = first-class audited action, authority per §14 Q2.

§6aBudget Lifecycle

This is the half of the module ProcurementExpress never had. A budget is not a spreadsheet uploaded once; it is a record that is proposed, approved, revised, drawn on, frozen and closed, and every one of those steps leaves a trail.

Templates

One BudgetTemplate per event type (conference, webinar, hybrid) seeded from the category list in §5, admin-editable, holding the lines a typical event of that type carries with either a default amount or a blank. Creating a budget from a template copies the lines; nothing links back afterwards, so editing a template never changes a live budget. Templates are the fastest way to make budgets comparable across events, which is what the cross-event report in §11 depends on.

Authoring

The editor groups lines by category, each with quantity, unit cost, transaction currency (FX snapshot per line, §7), tax code, service dates, optional supplier and notes. Totals, VAT and contingency recompute as lines change; nothing is saved until the owner saves. Where a benchmark source is set (§6c), the editor shows its planned and actual beside each category and, where lines match, beside each line, read-only (Phase 6; until then the source is recorded and nothing is shown).

Budget approval

A draft is submitted by the event owner (ORGANIZER), checked for completeness by the settle grant (Muthu: every category either has lines or is explicitly marked not applicable, contingency set, reporting currency set, expectedAttendance set), then approved on the same matrix as spend: Lina up to AED 1,000,000 of plannedExpenseTotal (contingency excluded), Medhat above, checked on the AED-converted total. Approval makes the version active and creates the event’s QuickBooks Class if it does not exist yet (§9.4); only an active version accepts spend requests.

Versions

Any change to an active budget’s planned amounts creates a new draft version, approved the same way; the previous version stays active until then, so spend never stops during a revision. Lines keep their lineKey across versions, which is what lets committed and actual figures follow the line rather than the version. A version compare view shows planned by line, old against new, with the delta and who approved it. A new version routes on its total, but Lina may approve a version whose delta is within her ceiling when Medhat approved the previous total (a setting Medhat can switch off).

Reallocation and contingency

Moving planned amount between lines inside the active version is a first-class audited action rather than a new version: the event owner may move, per version, up to 10% of a line’s approved planned amount in total, and never into a line carrying their own pending request; larger moves are approval requests on the AED matrix (§14 Q2). Contingency is a percentage of plannedExpenseTotal (which excludes it), default 10% (§14 Q8), carried as its own line; a draw from it is a spend request against that line, approved on the AED matrix like any other. A request that exceeds its line is the over-budget exception in §6; the requester may raise it against the contingency line instead, and an approved exception leaves the line’s remaining negative and shown as such.

Forecast and at-risk

forecastFinalAmount per line defaults to the larger of planned and committedOpen + actual (§7.7) and is editable by the owner with a reason. The budget forecast is the sum; the budget is flagged at risk when forecast exceeds plannedExpenseTotal plus contingency, on the dashboard and by a notification to the budget owner and the settle grant (the daily digest stays infrastructure-only by the August 2026 decision), so a problem is visible weeks before the invoices arrive rather than at close-out.

Freeze

Freeze on demand or on a date the owner sets (typically two weeks before the event): planned amounts and reallocation lock; spend requests within a line’s remaining run on the normal matrix; only an over-budget request on a frozen budget goes to Medhat as an exception with a reason (§8.5). Freeze is the point at which the plan stops moving and the actuals start to tell the story.

Close-out

After the event, once every bill for it is allocated (the unallocated bucket in §6 is empty or explicitly written off by the settle grant), the owner closes the budget: each line whose actual differs from planned by more than the threshold (10% or AED 5,000, whichever is larger; §14 Q13) requires a varianceNote, the close-out summary in §11 is generated with written-off bills as a reconciling item, the settle grant signs it off, recordedAttendance is written, and the budget moves to closed. Closed budgets are read-only; reopening is an ADMIN action with a reason, and the next close rewrites the budget’s EventFinancialSummary row. Archiving follows the event.

§6bRevenue & Event P&L (Phase 5)

Expense control alone tells an organiser what an event costs, not whether it made money. The revenue side uses data EA-SYS and the CRM already hold, so it is mostly reading, not entering.

  • Planned revenue lines (BudgetRevenueLine, §5): one per registration type (expected registrations × price, taken from the event’s registration types and tiers), one per sponsorship package or exhibitor (from the CRM pipeline, or typed), plus grants and other income.
  • Actuals are computed, never typed: paid registrations at their settled price (after promo codes and refunds) per registration type; won CRM deals linked to the event at their value; exhibitor and other lines manual with a reason. The figures refresh on the same cadence as the expense read-back.
  • Margin: revenue minus expense on the same ex-VAT basis, shown as amount and percentage against a targetMarginPercent set on the budget; below target flags the budget at risk the same way forecast overrun does.
  • Not in scope: posting revenue to accounting (it is already invoiced through EA-SYS and the CRM), deferred-revenue accounting, and cost allocation across events. If any of these is needed it is a later phase with its own decisions.

Computation rules (rev. 3.5, verified against the schema): the ex-VAT base of a registration is readRegistrationBasePrice (originalPrice − discountAmount; never the raw column, which is null on older rows and on some import paths) · refunds (Registration.refundedAmount, Payment.refundedAmount) are gross and are netted back to ex-VAT · a group registration carries its payment on Payment.groupId, the members still carry originalPrice · INCLUSIVE (sponsor-paid) registrations count zero attendee revenue, the sponsor’s money is the CRM deal; COMPLIMENTARY counts zero · currency comes from the ticket type, tier or payment, never from the event · sponsorship revenue is CrmDeal.dealValue where status = WON and the deal is linked to the event, not the deal’s product lines (value is manual by owner decision).

§6cBenchmarking (foundations Phase 1, feature Phase 6)

Added in rev. 3.4 (9 Sep 2026). A budget declares what it is measured against; the comparison is computed at read time from that source and never copied into the budget, so a corrected source corrects every comparison that reads it.

  • Source selection: EventBudget.benchmarkSourceType (ea_sys_budget | archive_summary | none) + benchmarkSourceId?, set at creation, changeable until the version is approved, audited. Repeat event: the default source is the closed budget of Event.previousEditionEventId (set by the clone route, editable in Settings), else the EventFinancialSummary row whose eventCode shares the code’s series prefix. First-time event: up to three candidates ranked by same eventType, same brand, recorded attendance within ±50% of expectedAttendance, most recent first; the organiser picks, the system never does.
  • Comparison rules: by lineKey when the source is an EA-SYS budget created from the same template, else by BudgetCategory.code. Source figures are converted to the new budget’s reporting currency at the source’s own snapshot rates (an archive summary at the rate on its asOf date), so a benchmark does not drift with today’s rate. Per-attendee figures divide by expectedAttendance on the new budget and by the recorded attendance on the source. Revenue and margin compare the same way once §6b lands.
  • Phase 1 foundations (fields and the index only, no comparison UI): EventBudget.expectedAttendance (required at submission), benchmarkSourceType / benchmarkSourceId, Event.previousEditionEventId?, fixed BudgetCategory.code values (§5), and the EventFinancialSummary index (§5, §13a) populated from the EventsAir archive export. These are what make the first year’s budgets comparable later; they cannot be retrofitted onto budgets written without them.
  • Phase 6 surfaces: the benchmark picker at creation with the ranked suggestions; a read-only benchmark column per category and matched line in the editor (replacing the “last year’s actual” reference in §6a); the approver’s delta line (total and per attendee); an “against benchmark” section in the close-out summary; the year-over-year report in §11. Nothing in §7, §8 or §9 changes.

§7Money, FX & Tax Rules

  1. Store 4 decimals, display 2; sum-vs-total drift ≤ 0.02 resolved at the total via banker's rounding — never by adjusting lines.
  2. FX rate snapshotted at line creation with source recorded; edits reuse the stored rate unless refresh_fx requested.
  3. Stale rate (> 24h, Asia/Dubai) blocks the write with a clear error and triggers a fetch. Rate ≤ 0 rejected at DB and API. Rates come from one named daily source chosen in Phase 0 (recommended: the Central Bank of the UAE published rates; §14 Q15) through an FxRateProvider interface beside AccountingProvider and a bounded worker job; historical rates are kept so a past date can be converted.
  4. reportingCurrency immutable once lines exist; changing it = clone to a new version, re-snapshot.
  5. Multicurrency is enabled on MME and required (confirmed 3 Sep 2026). The connector still verifies it at connection time and blocks pushes with an explicit error if it ever reports disabled. Exchange rate is carried on every foreign-currency push (QBO does not auto-apply ours).
  6. Negative amounts only on invoices/credits; planned lines ≥ 0. Zero-decimal currencies formatted via ISO 4217 minor units.
  7. Commitment convention (open-commitment): committedTotal = full PO value; committedOpen = PO value not yet billed. A matched bill moves value from committedOpen to actual. remaining = planned − committedOpen − actual per line; forecastFinalAmount defaults to max(planned, committedOpen + actual) and is editable by the budget owner with reason. actual converts at the bill’s own rate and paid at the payment’s; FX variance against the line’s snapshot is shown per line, never hidden in it. This is what makes "remaining" truthful on day one.
  8. Tax basis (signed off 3 Sep 2026): every planned / committed / actual / paid figure is ex-VAT; VAT (UAE 5%, recoverable) is carried in taxAmount fields at every level. QBO pushes use GlobalTaxCalculation = TaxExcluded with the mapped tax code per line. Payments arrive gross and are netted back as in §6. Tax codes include reverse-charge (foreign suppliers) and out-of-scope; foreign VAT that is not recoverable in the UAE (for example KSA VAT on a Riyadh event) is part of the line amount, not taxAmount.
  9. Currencies: budgets may report in AED, USD, EUR, GBP or SAR (§14 Q7). The organization base currency is AED; approval bands, ceilings, and cross-event dashboards are evaluated in AED using the line's snapshot rate. All rules enforced as DB CHECK constraints as well as in the service layer.

§8Approval Edge Cases

  1. Amount edited while approval pending → pending request auto-superseded, fresh request generated, audit links old → new.
  2. Requester = approver → hard 403, no role bypasses it. A request raised by an approver routes to the tier above them (Lina’s own requests go to Medhat). Delegate ≠ requester.
  3. Approver inaction 48h → delegate; further 48h → next tier. Escalations audited and notified. Delegates per-role, overrideable per-user (§14 Q5). At launch the chain is Lina → Medhat; Lina’s delegate and next tier are both Medhat, so her inaction passes to him at 48h. Medhat is the top, so a request waiting on him is reminded daily rather than escalated; there is no standby for him in v1 (decision 9 Sep 2026).
  4. Concurrent decisions → optimistic locking, stale client refetches.
  5. On a frozen budget, requests within a line’s remaining follow the normal matrix; only over-budget requests need Medhat’s override with a reason; UI warns loudly.
  6. Rejecting a new budget version does not auto-cancel posted commitments (the previous version stays active) — they remain valid until the settle grant explicitly cancels them (which closes the QBO PO).
  7. Partial approvals not supported in v1.
  8. The top approver never requests. Medhat approves and never raises a request (8 Sep 2026). Enforced rather than assumed: the final approver’s account holds procurement.approve only, never procurement.request, and a spend request from the final approver is refused at submission, so requester ≠ approver can never leave a request with no approver.

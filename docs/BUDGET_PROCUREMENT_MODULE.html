1The plan in one page

Every MMG event gets a budget that stays true from the first plan to the final close. Line by line it shows what we planned to spend, what we have promised to suppliers, what has been billed, and what has been paid. Buying anything goes through a request, an approval and a purchase order, so the budget updates itself instead of depending on someone remembering to update a spreadsheet.

Purchase orders are posted to QuickBooks. Bills and payments that Muthu records in QuickBooks flow back into the budget. Once that is working, the revenue side is added: registrations and sponsorship money against expenses, so each event shows its margin.

What changes for people

  • Staff raise spend requests inside EA-SYS, where they already log in, instead of ProcurementExpress.
  • Lina and Medhat approve from an inbox that reminds and escalates on its own.
  • Muthu keeps recording bills and payments in QuickBooks exactly as today. The module reads them back.
  • Event owners get one dashboard per event and one view across every event in the year.
  • ProcurementExpress is cancelled after the first clean month-end close on the new module.

The one rule behind everything: EA-SYS is where decisions are made and recorded. QuickBooks is where postings land. Anything changed directly in QuickBooks is shown as a difference to resolve, never quietly copied over.

2The four numbers on every line

Each budget line (say "AV and production" for one event) carries four figures. They are the whole point of the module.

Planned

What the approved budget says we will spend on this line.

Committed

Purchase orders issued and not yet billed. The promise we have made to a supplier.

Actual

Bills received against those purchase orders. As a bill arrives, value moves from committed to actual.

Paid

Money that has actually left the bank, read back from QuickBooks.

Remaining = Planned minus open commitments minus Actual. This is why "remaining" is truthful from day one: a purchase order reduces it the moment it is approved, not weeks later when the bill turns up.

Forecast is the system's estimate of where the line will finish. When the forecast for the whole event is higher than planned plus contingency, the budget is flagged at risk on the dashboard and in the daily email, weeks before the invoices arrive.

  • Every figure is excluding VAT, with the VAT amount shown beside it. Finance signed this off on 3 September 2026.
  • A budget is kept in one currency of its choice (AED, USD, EUR, GBP or SAR). Exchange rates are fixed on each line when it is written, so a rate change later never rewrites history.
  • Approval limits and the cross-event view are always in AED.
  • Bills that arrive for an event with no purchase order behind them are shown in a separate "unallocated" bucket, so nothing is hidden and finance can see the gap between the module and the QuickBooks profit and loss.

3How a budget lives

A budget is not a spreadsheet uploaded once. It is proposed, approved, revised, drawn on, frozen and closed, and each step leaves a trail. ProcurementExpress never had this half.

  1. Start from a template. One template per event type (conference, webinar, hybrid) with the usual lines already listed. Editing a template later never changes a live budget.
  2. Fill in the lines. Quantity, unit cost, currency, VAT code, dates, supplier if known. Totals, VAT and contingency recalculate as you type. Where a past event of the same kind exists, last year's actual is shown beside each category for reference.
  3. Submit for approval. Finance checks it is complete (every category has lines or is marked not applicable, contingency and currency set). Then it is approved on the same limits as spending: Lina up to AED 1,000,000 of planned expense, Medhat above.
  4. Active. Only an active budget accepts spend requests.
  5. Revise through versions. Changing planned amounts creates a new version that goes through approval. The old version stays active until then, so spending never stops during a revision. Committed and actual figures follow each line into the new version. A compare view shows old against new, the difference, and who approved it.
  6. Move money between lines. An event owner may move up to 10% of a line on their own; finance may move any amount. Every move is recorded. Spending from contingency is a request against the contingency line and always needs finance approval.
  7. Freeze before the event. On demand or on a set date (typically two weeks out) the plan locks. Spending continues; approvals on a frozen budget need the top approver and a written reason.
  8. Close out after the event. Once every bill is allocated, the owner closes the budget. Any line more than 10% or AED 5,000 off plan (whichever is larger) needs a written explanation. A close-out summary is produced, finance signs it off, and the budget becomes read-only. Reopening needs an admin and a reason.

4Buying something

This is the daily flow, so its screens get the most polish. From request to paid bill:

  1. Raise the request. The requester picks the event and the budget line. The form shows what is left on that line, open commitments, past dealings with the supplier, and who will approve.
  2. Attach quotes. A new supplier can be proposed on the same form. Finance approves the supplier (tax number, terms, currency) before any purchase order can be issued to them.
  3. Budget check. Within budget: goes to Lina, or to Medhat above AED 1,000,000. Over budget: blocked and routed to Medhat as an exception. It is never silently allowed through.
  4. Approval. The approver decides from the inbox. Reminder after 24 hours, passed to a delegate after 48, escalated to the next level after 96. Medhat is the top of the chain, so a request waiting on him is reminded daily instead.
  5. Purchase order. Approval creates the purchase order, posts it to QuickBooks, and emails a PDF to the supplier using the same layout as our invoices and quotes.
  6. Receiving. The requester (or finance) marks the order partly or fully received.
  7. Bill and payment. Muthu records the bill and the payment in QuickBooks as today. Within 24 hours the line shows the new actual and paid figures.
  8. Changes after approval. A changed amount is re-approved for the difference only. A purchase order already posted is cancelled and re-issued rather than edited, so QuickBooks and EA-SYS never disagree about it.

5Who does what

Nobody gets a new login. Procurement permissions are added on top of the EA-SYS account people already have.

WhoWhat they can do
Any staff member with the "request" permissionRaise and submit their own spend requests, attach quotes, propose a new supplier, see what is left on the lines they can request against, mark their own orders as received.
Event owner (organiser)Write and revise the budget for their events, move up to 10% between lines, watch requests on their events. No accounting access.
LinaApproves budgets and spend up to AED 1,000,000. Medhat is her delegate when she is away.
MedhatApproves anything above AED 1,000,000 and every over-budget exception. Final approver on everything. Never raises a request, and the system refuses one from his account, so a request can never be left with nobody able to approve it.
Muthu (finance)Approves new suppliers, checks budgets for completeness, records bills and payments in QuickBooks, reviews unallocated bills, uses the reconciliation view, dashboards and exports. Not an approver of spend.
AdminsEverything above, plus the QuickBooks connection, account mappings, overrides, close-out reopening and resolving differences with QuickBooks.

Hard rules, with no way round them:

  • Nobody can approve their own request. Where a second approval is required, it must be a different person.
  • A delegate can never be the person who raised the request.
  • Limits are checked in AED at the moment of the decision.
  • Supplier bank details and tax numbers are treated like passport and bank details in the speaker reimbursement module: visible to finance and admins only, never quoted in the audit trail.

6Revenue and margin (Phase 5)

Controlling expenses tells you what an event cost, not whether it made money. The revenue side uses figures EA-SYS and the CRM already hold, so it is mostly reading, not typing.

  • Planned revenue: one line per registration type (expected numbers times price, taken from the event's registration types), one per sponsorship package or exhibitor (from the CRM pipeline), plus grants and other income.
  • Actual revenue is calculated, never typed: paid registrations at the price actually paid (after promo codes and refunds), and won sponsorship deals at their value. Only "other" income is entered by hand.
  • Margin: revenue minus expense on the same excluding-VAT basis, shown as an amount and a percentage against the target set on the budget. Below target flags the event at risk, the same way an expense overrun does.
  • Not included: posting revenue to QuickBooks (it is already invoiced through EA-SYS and the CRM), deferred revenue accounting, and sharing costs across events. Each of those would be a later decision.

7QuickBooks

  • One company. Everything posts to the single MME QuickBooks file. There is no separate entity dimension.
  • Event code is the QuickBooks Class. Finance already tracks events this way, so event spend stays reportable inside QuickBooks. Every purchase order line carries the event code. In the first phase the event code becomes unique across MMG and cannot change once a budget or order uses it.
  • Multi-currency is switched on in QuickBooks and required. The module double-checks this when it connects.
  • We only create in QuickBooks, never edit. Approved orders are posted as purchase orders. A change means cancel and re-issue. New suppliers are created in QuickBooks the first time they get an order.
  • We read back every night, and on demand: purchase orders, bills and payments. A bill linked to our order updates actual and paid. A bill for an event with no order behind it goes to the unallocated bucket. A payment updates paid.
  • Changes made directly in QuickBooks (an order edited, closed or deleted there) are flagged as "diverged" and shown to finance and admins to resolve: accept the QuickBooks version, or cancel and re-issue ours.
  • Connection health. The QuickBooks connection expires periodically. The module warns ahead of time and finance re-authorises in a couple of clicks. An Integration Monitor screen shows the last sync, failures, the retry queue and the unallocated count. Nothing fails silently.
  • Proof before we rely on it. In the first week a short trial against QuickBooks confirms the connection, the Class lookup, a purchase order with an event code, and reading a linked bill back.

8Screens and reports

Budget screens, used every day:

  • Budget dashboard per event: planned, committed, actual, paid, remaining, forecast, at-risk flag, unallocated bills, contingency left, open approvals, failed syncs.
  • Budget editor with running totals, last year's reference figures and the contingency panel.
  • Version compare: old against new, line by line, with the approver.
  • Move-between-lines and contingency actions, with the authority rules built in.
  • Freeze and close-out, with variance explanations and the close-out summary.
  • Event profit and loss (Phase 5) and the cross-event view: every event this year by brand with planned, forecast, actual and margin.

Procurement screens: spend request form with a live budget side panel · approval inbox (requests, budgets, exceptions, contingency draws, supplier approvals) · purchase order list with sync and receiving status, linked bills and the PDF · supplier list with the proposed-supplier queue · mapping admin · Integration Monitor. English first; Arabic later, platform-wide.

Reports: planned versus committed versus actual versus paid by event, with unallocated bills shown separately · variance by category with the owner's explanations · forecast and at-risk list across events · contingency usage · close-out summary · cross-event comparison by year and brand · event profit and loss (Phase 5) · supplier spend by event · open commitments · approval turnaround. All exportable to CSV and Excel.

Expectation to set with finance: until the later "invoice exception" phase, the module's actual figure for an event will sit below the QuickBooks profit and loss, because bills without a purchase order are counted in the unallocated bucket rather than on a line. The gap is visible, not hidden.

10Leaving ProcurementExpress

  • Everything ProcurementExpress does today that we rely on (budgets, approval routing, purchase order PDFs to suppliers, the monthly finance reports) is in the first release. Muthu will list the exact reports pulled today so nothing is missed.
  • Suppliers are exported and matched to existing QuickBooks vendors by tax number, then by name. Anything ambiguous is resolved by hand on a screen.
  • Open purchase orders already exist in QuickBooks, so they are linked, not re-created. The historical export is archived, not imported.
  • Live event budgets are rebuilt with their current committed amounts, so "remaining" is right on day one.
  • ProcurementExpress's own QuickBooks connection is disconnected only after ours is verified.
  • Parallel run with a pilot group, then one clean month-end close, then the subscription is cancelled.

11EventsAir

  • Budgets attach only to events that live in EA-SYS. EA-SYS has created every event since June 2026.
  • EventsAir history is archived and indexed, never imported into the live system (decision of 2 September 2026). An early export of past events, registrations and financial summaries goes to the backup bucket, with a searchable index, so it does not depend on how long EventsAir access lasts.
  • When a budget is created, its event code is checked against that index. A code that clashes with a historical event is refused, so year-on-year comparison by code stays possible without importing anything.
  • How long EventsAir stays in use is Medhat's decision. Nothing in this module depends on a date.

14What done looks like

  • A budget can be created from a template, filled in with correct currency and VAT handling, submitted and approved on the AED limits. A new version keeps every line's committed and actual figures, and the compare view shows each change with its approver.
  • Moving money and drawing on contingency follow the authority rules and are recorded. The at-risk flag fires before any bill arrives. Freeze locks the plan while spending continues.
  • A budget cannot close with unallocated bills. Every line over the variance threshold carries an explanation. The close-out summary reconciles to QuickBooks. Closed budgets are read-only.
  • The year view lists every event by brand with planned, forecast and actual, from the same figures the event dashboards show.
  • A spend request with quotes can be raised against an active budget. Over-budget requests are blocked and routed. A proposed supplier is approved before an order is issued.
  • Nobody approves their own request. Limits are checked in AED. Inaction is reminded and escalated with notifications.
  • Approved requests become purchase orders, post to QuickBooks with the event code, and the PDF reaches the supplier.
  • Bills and payments recorded in QuickBooks update actual and paid within 24 hours. Non-order bills appear as unallocated. An order edited in QuickBooks is flagged.
  • Every important action (submission, approval, amount change, override, sync fix, close-out) is in the audit trail with before, after and reason. Sync failures are visible and can be re-run by hand. Nothing fails silently.
  • Migration is complete with linked (not re-created) orders, and one finance month-end close has passed on real data.
  • Phase 5: planned revenue by registration type and sponsorship package; actual revenue computed from paid registrations and won deals with no manual entry; margin against target on the event and cross-event views.

15Words used in this document

Spend request
The form a staff member fills in to ask for money to be spent. It carries quotes and goes through approval.
Purchase order (PO)
The approved commitment to a supplier. It is what "committed" counts, and what gets posted to QuickBooks and emailed to the supplier.
Committed, open commitment
Purchase order value not yet billed. As bills arrive, value moves from committed to actual.
Actual
Bills received against our purchase orders, matched line by line.
Unallocated
A bill for an event that has no purchase order behind it. Counted separately so it is not lost and not hidden.
Excluding VAT (ex-VAT)
Amounts shown without the 5% VAT, which is recorded beside them. UAE VAT is recoverable, so this is the basis finance asked for.
Class (QuickBooks)
The label QuickBooks uses to tag transactions to an event. Our event code is that label.
Read-back
The nightly and on-demand pull of purchase orders, bills and payments from QuickBooks into the budget.
Diverged
A purchase order that was changed directly in QuickBooks so it no longer matches ours. Shown for finance to resolve.
Contingency
A reserve line, 10% of planned expense by default, that only finance can approve spending from.
Freeze
The point, typically two weeks before the event, when the plan stops changing and only spending continues.
Close-out
The final step after the event: all bills allocated, variances explained, summary signed off, budget made read-only.
Parallel run
A period when the pilot group uses the new module while ProcurementExpress is still available, before it is cancelled.

§2Governing Principles

  1. The budget is the product. Every screen answers one question, "where does this event stand against its plan", and everything else (spend requests, purchase orders, the accounting connector, the reports) exists to keep that answer truthful. A feature that does not improve the answer is out of scope.
  2. EA-SYS is the system of record — workflow, approvals, budget logic, event metadata, audit history, procurement decisions. A change made directly in QuickBooks is a divergence to be surfaced, never silently absorbed.
  3. QuickBooks is a posting target, not the architectural centre. QBO-specific logic lives only in the provider adapter and mapping config.
  4. No core table, screen, or business rule named after QuickBooks. Generic names: Commitment.externalDocumentId, AccountMapping.providerName.
  5. Event code is the reconciliation key on every transactional object.
  6. Replaceable connector: AccountingProvider interface; QuickBooksProvider first.
  7. One money basis everywhere: ex-VAT, in the budget's reporting currency, 4 decimals stored (§7).
  8. Nothing fails silently. Every sync failure, divergence, or unallocated amount is visible on a screen and in the audit log.
  9. Supplier and payment data is classified. Tax registration numbers, bank details and payment records are financial data about third parties. They follow the platform’s reimbursement boundary: staff-only reads, redacted outside it, never quoted in audit diffs. The security-and-privacy posture document (the one handed to health-authority clients) is updated before the pilot.

§3Architecture

  • EA-SYS module: src/procurement/, one-way imports procurement → core (CRM/HR pattern). Next.js 16 / TypeScript / Prisma / PostgreSQL.
  • Two new core primitives, built in this project, owned by the platform:
  • src/lib/outbox/ — transactional outbox table, drainer in the worker container, idempotency keys, exponential backoff, dead-letter with alerting. EA-SYS has no outbox today (April 2026 resilience audit: not implemented); procurement is its first consumer, SES sends and Stripe follow-ups are the next. The drainer is a worker job under the expiring-lease singleton the worker already uses.
  • src/lib/approvals/ — ApprovalWorkflowDefinition / ApprovalRequest / ApprovalStep, amount-banded routing, escalation, optimistic locking, requester ≠ approver. Procurement is the first consumer; the HR module (leave approvals) is the second. Placing these in src/procurement/ would make them unreachable to HR under the one-way import rule.
  • Services layer shared by REST + MCP + cron, errors-as-values, every failure path logged, AuditLog with source tagging — platform conventions apply unchanged.
  • Tenancy: organizationId on every table. RLS policy files ship in prisma/rls/ with the same change, together with the tenancy-harness assertions and the CI gate entries; they are applied by the harness and the platform bootstrap and never inside a Prisma migration (master keeps a database with zero RLS objects). The worker (outbox drainer, CDC poll) reads the organization off each row and runs it in that tenant’s lane, the pattern every existing worker job uses, so the first outbox row does not fail under RLS.
  • Database pool: web and worker each hold their own Prisma pool of 10 on the shared pooler (production measured 24 of 90 connections). The drainer and the CDC poll are batch-bounded worker jobs, so two new background consumers cannot exhaust the worker’s pool or starve the registration desk.
  • Capability grants: two nullable columns on User (procurementRequest, procurementApproveCeilingAed), carried in the JWT and re-validated on the existing 5-minute cycle, exactly like the HR module’s hrAccess grant. The Edge middleware that confines CRM_USER to /crm and HR_USER to /hr gains a /procurement exemption for grant-holders, and every procurement route opts grant-holders in through the write guard’s allow-list, so a role that is refused by default stays refused unless the grant is present.
  • Provider adapter: src/procurement/providers/AccountingProvider.ts — syncVendors, createVendor, createPurchaseOrder, voidPurchaseOrder, createBill (Phase 2), getPurchaseOrder, getBillStatus, getPaymentStatus, syncChartOfAccounts, syncTaxCodes, syncClassesOrLocations, healthCheck — implemented by QuickBooksProvider.
  • MCP: procurement tools added to the per-domain tool set (spend-request creation/status via the in-app agent and claude.ai) for signed-in users holding a grant. Organization API keys carry no user and are admin-equivalent everywhere else, so they get no procurement tools in v1.

§4Roles & Permissions

Procurement capabilities are additive grants on top of a user’s primary role, not a new exclusive role. An ORGANIZER who raises POs keeps ORGANIZER and gains procurement.request. SUBMITTER/REVIEWER remain untouched (abstracts). Mechanism: §3 (columns on User, JWT-carried, allow-list opt-in per route).

Grant / role Capabilities
procurement.request Create/submit own spend requests, attach quotes, propose a new supplier, view own request history, see available budget on lines they can request against, mark own commitments received. Almost every employee already has an EA-SYS account (CRM_USER, MEMBER, ORGANIZER…); this grant is added on top of whatever role they hold — no new provisioning wave (§14 Q10, answered).
procurement.approve (with an AED ceiling) Approval inbox, decide within authority ceiling. Launch matrix (§14 Q3): Lina approves up to AED 1,000,000; anything above goes to Medhat, who is unlimited and the final approver on everything. Lina's escalation delegate is Medhat; Medhat has no delegate — requests above Lina's ceiling wait for him.
ORGANIZER Event-scoped budget authoring (propose lines), monitor requests for their events. No accounting access.
MEMBER (finance-capable) Muthu — settlement: records bills and payments against POs in QuickBooks, reconciliation view, supplier approval, unallocated-actuals review, dashboards, exports. Not an approver.
ADMIN / SUPER_ADMIN Full module access; connector settings, mappings, overrides, close-out, divergence resolution.

Hard rules: requester ≠ approver on the same entity, enforced in the approvals service regardless of role, no bypass. Where requiresSecondApproval is set, the second approver must be a different person from the first. A delegate can never be the requester. Approval authority is an amount ceiling in AED (organization base currency, §7.9), checked at decision time on the AED-converted amount. Enforcement at all 3 layers (guard, proxy.ts middleware, UI).

§5Domain Model (Prisma entities, key fields)

All monetary values Decimal (NUMERIC), never float; all stored ex-VAT with a sibling tax amount. All tables carry organizationId (RLS), createdAt/updatedAt, row versioning where mutable. Number sequences (requestNo, commitmentNo) reuse the platform’s atomic counter pattern (one organization-scoped counter row per sequence, incremented inside the creating transaction), the mechanism behind registration serials; invoice numbers are per event and do not fit.

  • EventBudget: eventId → EA-SYS Event (nullable, reserved for one entity-level operational budget — §14 Q4), eventCode (copied from Event.code, which Phase 1 makes unique per organization and immutable once a budget or commitment references it; today it is a free, non-unique, name-derived string), versionNo (monotonic per event, exactly one active version), brand? (MMG Experts | MedCom | MedULive — reporting label only, never pushed to accounting: MME is the single QuickBooks company), reportingCurrency (immutable once lines exist), contingencyPercent/Amount, plannedExpenseTotal, ownerUserId, financeOwnerUserId, status draft → under_review → approved → active → frozen → closed → archived, at_risk flag. (Phase 5, §6b: plannedRevenueTotal, actualRevenueTotal, targetMarginPercent.)
  • BudgetTemplate / BudgetTemplateLine: per event type; seeds lines when a budget is created. Admin-editable.
  • BudgetCategory: hierarchical (max depth 3), code ("VENUE.AV"), type (expense | revenue), defaultAccountMappingId, owningDepartmentId? (§14 Q1). Seeded with Doc 2's list (Venue, F&B, AV & Production, Speakers & Faculty, Travel, Accommodation, Staffing, Marketing, Printing & Signage, Registration Ops, Technology, Compliance & Accreditation, Translation, Misc, Contingency).
  • BudgetLine: lineKey (UUID, stable across versions — copied on version clone; Commitments and reports resolve through it), category, description, supplierId?, qty, unitCost, transactionCurrency, fxRateToReporting + fxRateSource (snapshot at creation), planned / committedOpen / committedTotal / actual / paid (ex-VAT, reporting currency), taxAmountPlanned, forecastFinalAmount, tax code, service dates, soft delete, status.   • BudgetRevenueLine (Phase 5, §6b): lineKey, source (registration_type | sponsorship | exhibition | grant | other), ticketTypeId? (EA-SYS registration type), crmDealId? (CRM sponsorship deal), description, plannedQty, plannedUnitAmount, plannedAmount, actualAmount (computed: paid registrations at their settled price for a registration type; won deal value for a sponsorship; manual for other), currency + FX snapshot as for expense lines, ex-VAT with tax alongside. §6Core Workflows

Budget: create for event (optionally from template) → auto-version → enter lines (FX snapshot per line) → totals + contingency computed → submit → approval per workflow rules → active. New version clones lines with their lineKey; commitments follow the key.

Spend request (the daily-use flow — polish this UI): requester picks event + active budget → side panel shows available budget on the category/line, current open commitments, suggested approval route, vendor history → submits with quotes (and a proposed supplier if new) → budget availability check (ex-VAT, reporting currency): within budget → Lina (Medhat above AED 1M); over budget → blocked and routed as an exception to Medhat, never silent → approval (ceiling checked in AED) → Commitment created with lines → outbox → posted to QBO as Purchase Order → externalDocumentId stored, status posted → PO PDF generated and emailed to the supplier (reusing the invoice / quote PDF layout in src/lib/pdf/document-layout.ts: logo, bill-to boxes, line table, totals; the certificate pipeline is a text overlay on an uploaded background and does not fit), send recorded in audit.

Supplier onboarding: a proposed supplier rides along with the request; finance approves the supplier (TRN, terms, currency) before or as part of the request's finance step. Adapter creates the vendor in QBO on first commitment; externalVendorId stored.

Approver notifications: email on assignment, reminder at 24h, delegate at 48h, next tier at 96h (§8.3). Without push, escalation becomes the normal path.

Receiving: requester (or finance) marks a commitment partially/fully received; fulfillmentStatus drives the open-commitment view alongside bill linkage.

Read-back (v1, read-only): nightly + on-demand CDC poll pulls PurchaseOrders, Bills and BillPayments changed since cursor. - Bill lines with LinkedTxn.TxnLineId → that CommitmentLine's lineKey (explicit). Header-only linkage → proportional by PO line value (proportional), flagged for finance override. Amounts that do not reconcile → mismatch, surfaced. - Bills and Purchases carrying the event dimension but no PO link → recorded as unallocated actuals for the event (bucket visible in v1; assignment workflow Phase 2). - BillPayments → PaymentRecord (gross); line paid = gross × (invoice ex-VAT ÷ invoice gross). - Bill voided in QBO → local void + committedOpen restored + event emitted. - PO edited/closed/deleted in QBO → Commitment diverged, surfaced in Integration Monitor for ADMIN/finance resolution (accept QBO / void-and-recreate). - CDC cursor older than the 30-day window → automatic full re-list by date range, logged.

Post-approval changes: amount change after approval → new approval scoped to the delta, reason captured, audited; posted PO → void and recreate (create-only policy). Cancelling a posted Commitment voids the QBO PO through the adapter. Moving budget between lines = first-class audited action, authority per §14 Q2.

§6aBudget Lifecycle

This is the half of the module ProcurementExpress never had. A budget is not a spreadsheet uploaded once; it is a record that is proposed, approved, revised, drawn on, frozen and closed, and every one of those steps leaves a trail.

Templates

One BudgetTemplate per event type (conference, webinar, hybrid) seeded from the category list in §5, admin-editable, holding the lines a typical event of that type carries with either a default amount or a blank. Creating a budget from a template copies the lines; nothing links back afterwards, so editing a template never changes a live budget. Templates are the fastest way to make budgets comparable across events, which is what the cross-event report in §11 depends on.

Authoring

The editor groups lines by category, each with quantity, unit cost, transaction currency (FX snapshot per line, §7), tax code, service dates, optional supplier and notes. Totals, VAT and contingency recompute as lines change; nothing is saved until the owner saves. Where the archive index (§13a) holds a past event with the same code prefix or type, the editor shows last year’s actual beside each category as a reference figure, read-only.

Budget approval

A draft is submitted by the event owner (ORGANIZER), checked for completeness by finance (Muthu: every category either has lines or is explicitly marked not applicable, contingency set, reporting currency set), then approved on the same matrix as spend: Lina up to AED 1,000,000 of planned expense, Medhat above, checked on the AED-converted total. Approval makes the version active; only an active version accepts spend requests.

Versions

Any change to an active budget’s planned amounts creates a new draft version, approved the same way; the previous version stays active until then, so spend never stops during a revision. Lines keep their lineKey across versions, which is what lets committed and actual figures follow the line rather than the version. A version compare view shows planned by line, old against new, with the delta and who approved it.

Reallocation and contingency

Moving planned amount between lines inside the active version is a first-class audited action rather than a new version: the event owner may move up to 10% of a line’s planned amount, finance any amount (§14 Q2, recommended answer adopted until changed). Contingency is a percentage of planned expense, default 10% (§14 Q8), carried as its own line; a draw from it is a spend request against that line and needs finance approval regardless of amount. A request that cannot fit its line or contingency is the over-budget exception in §6.

Forecast and at-risk

forecastFinalAmount per line defaults to committedTotal + actual − overlap (§7.7) and is editable by the owner with a reason. The budget forecast is the sum; the budget is flagged at risk when forecast exceeds planned plus contingency, on the dashboard and in the daily digest, so a problem is visible weeks before the invoices arrive rather than at close-out.

Freeze

Freeze on demand or on a date the owner sets (typically two weeks before the event): planned amounts and reallocation lock, spend requests still run, approvals on a frozen budget need the highest-tier override with a reason (§8.5). Freeze is the point at which the plan stops moving and the actuals start to tell the story.

Close-out

After the event, once every bill for it is allocated (the unallocated bucket in §6 is empty or explicitly written off by finance), the owner closes the budget: each line whose actual differs from planned by more than the threshold (10% or AED 5,000, whichever is larger; §14 Q13) requires a varianceNote, the close-out summary in §11 is generated, and the budget moves to closed. Closed budgets are read-only; reopening is an ADMIN action with a reason. Archiving follows the event.

§6bRevenue & Event P&L (Phase 5)

Expense control alone tells an organiser what an event costs, not whether it made money. The revenue side uses data EA-SYS and the CRM already hold, so it is mostly reading, not entering.

  • Planned revenue lines (BudgetRevenueLine, §5): one per registration type (expected registrations × price, taken from the event’s registration types and tiers), one per sponsorship package or exhibitor (from the CRM pipeline, or typed), plus grants and other income.
  • Actuals are computed, never typed: paid registrations at their settled price (after promo codes and refunds) per registration type; won CRM deals linked to the event at their value; exhibitor and other lines manual with a reason. The figures refresh on the same cadence as the expense read-back.
  • Margin: revenue minus expense on the same ex-VAT basis, shown as amount and percentage against a targetMarginPercent set on the budget; below target flags the budget at risk the same way forecast overrun does.
  • Not in scope: posting revenue to accounting (it is already invoiced through EA-SYS and the CRM), deferred-revenue accounting, and cost allocation across events. If any of these is needed it is a later phase with its own decisions.

§7Money, FX & Tax Rules

  1. Store 4 decimals, display 2; sum-vs-total drift ≤ 0.02 resolved at the total via banker's rounding — never by adjusting lines.
  2. FX rate snapshotted at line creation with source recorded; edits reuse the stored rate unless refresh_fx requested.
  3. Stale rate (> 24h, Asia/Dubai) blocks the write with a clear error and triggers a fetch. Rate ≤ 0 rejected at DB and API.
  4. reportingCurrency immutable once lines exist; changing it = clone to a new version, re-snapshot.
  5. Multicurrency is enabled on MME and required (confirmed 3 Sep 2026). The connector still verifies it at connection time and blocks pushes with an explicit error if it ever reports disabled. Exchange rate is carried on every foreign-currency push (QBO does not auto-apply ours).
  6. Negative amounts only on invoices/credits; planned lines ≥ 0. Zero-decimal currencies formatted via ISO 4217 minor units.
  7. Commitment convention (open-commitment): committedTotal = full PO value; committedOpen = PO value not yet billed. A matched bill moves value from committedOpen to actual. remaining = planned − committedOpen − actual per line; forecastFinalAmount defaults to committedTotal + actual − overlap and is editable by the budget owner with reason. This is what makes "remaining" truthful on day one.
  8. Tax basis (signed off 3 Sep 2026): every planned / committed / actual / paid figure is ex-VAT; VAT (UAE 5%, recoverable) is carried in taxAmount fields at every level. QBO pushes use GlobalTaxCalculation = TaxExcluded with the mapped tax code per line. Payments arrive gross and are netted back as in §6.
  9. Currencies: budgets may report in AED, USD, EUR, GBP or SAR (§14 Q7). The organization base currency is AED; approval bands, ceilings, and cross-event dashboards are evaluated in AED using the line's snapshot rate. All rules enforced as DB CHECK constraints as well as in the service layer.

§8Approval Edge Cases

  1. Amount edited while approval pending → pending request auto-superseded, fresh request generated, audit links old → new.
  2. Requester = approver → hard 403, no role bypasses it. Second approver ≠ first approver. Delegate ≠ requester.
  3. Approver inaction 48h → delegate; further 48h → next tier. Escalations audited and notified. Delegates per-role, overrideable per-user (§14 Q5). At launch the chain is Lina → Medhat; Medhat is the top, so a request waiting on him is reminded daily rather than escalated.
  4. Concurrent decisions → optimistic locking, stale client refetches.
  5. Approvals on frozen budgets require override with reason, highest tier only; UI warns loudly.
  6. Rejecting a budget does not auto-cancel posted commitments — they remain valid until finance explicitly cancels (which voids the QBO PO).
  7. Partial approvals not supported in v1.
  8. The top approver never requests. Medhat approves and never raises a request (8 Sep 2026). Enforced rather than assumed: the final approver’s account holds procurement.approve only, never procurement.request, and a spend request from the final approver is refused at submission, so requester ≠ approver can never leave a request with no approver.

  4. Dimensions: MME is the only QuickBooks company, so there is no entity dimension. The event code is the QuickBooks Class — confirmed 3 Sep 2026; finance already tracks events this way. Permanent 4xx → SyncLog + Integration Monitor.

§15v1 Acceptance Criteria

  • Budget: create a budget from a template, author lines with FX-correct, ex-VAT amounts and contingency, submit it and approve it on the AED matrix; a new version preserves committed and actual figures per line and the compare view shows every delta with its approver.
  • Control: reallocation and contingency draws respect the authority rules and are audited; forecast per line rolls up to the budget and the at-risk flag fires on the dashboard and in the daily digest before any bill arrives; freeze locks the plan while spend continues.
  • Close-out: a budget cannot close with unallocated actuals; every line over the variance threshold carries an explanation; the close-out summary reconciles to the QuickBooks figures for the event to the amount stated in §11; closed budgets are read-only.
  • Cross-event: the year view lists every event by brand with planned, forecast and actual from the same figures the event dashboards show.
  • Submit a spend request with quotes against an active budget; the system blocks or exception-routes over-budget requests; a proposed supplier is approved before a PO can be posted.
  • Requester can never approve own request; second approver is independent; approvals respect AED ceilings and escalate on inaction with notifications.
  • Approved requests create Commitments with lines; Commitments post to QuickBooks as POs via the adapter; external ID stored; event code preserved end-to-end; PO PDF reaches the supplier.
  • Bills and payments entered in QBO against our POs update actual (PO-matched) and paid within 24h; non-PO bills for an event appear as unallocated actuals; a PO edited in QBO is flagged as diverged.
  • Dashboard shows planned / committed open / actual (PO-matched) / paid / remaining per event; sync failures are visible and manually reprocessable; nothing fails silently.
  • Audit log captures every critical action (submission, approval, amount change, override, sync resolution, divergence resolution, close-out) with before/after and reason.
  • RLS active on all module tables and honoured by the worker; migration completed with linked (not re-pushed) POs; one finance month-end close passed on real data.
  • Event.code is unique per organization and every module object carries it; the security-and-privacy posture document describes supplier financial data before the pilot; no procurement data is reachable through an organization API key; a spend request from the final approver is refused at submission.
  • Phase 5: planned revenue by registration type and sponsorship package; actual revenue computed from paid registrations and won CRM deals with no manual entry for those sources; margin against target on the event P&L and the cross-event view.

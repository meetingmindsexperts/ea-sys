Webinar Streaming

How a webinar gets from the speaker's camera to thousands of attendees on our page: the architecture, the system design, and the rules (algorithms) each part follows. Written Nov 5, 2026, after the first full test webinar. For running a webinar, see the operator guide (public/user-guide.html §11); for setup history, see docs/LIVE_STREAMING.md and docs/WEBINAR_EVENTS.md.

1. The picture in one line

The speakers present in Zoom. Zoom pushes one video stream to our own media server. Our server cuts it into four-second pieces, and every attendee's browser downloads those pieces from our website. Zoom never sees the attendees, so the audience size does not cost Zoom seats.

Speakers in ZoomZoom webinar, host + panelists
→
Zoom live streamone RTMP push to our box, port 1935
→
MediaMTXcontainer ea-sys-mediamtx: RTMP in, HLS out (4 s pieces)
→
nginx /stream/optional CDN in front (HLS_CDN_BASE)
→
Attendee page/e/{slug}/session/{id}, the LivePlayer

Beside the video, the same page runs a lighter loop for everything else: the waiting room, Q&A, polls, presence and the end-of-webinar survey all talk to our web app by polling small JSON routes.

The rest of this document is mostly about the custom stream, since that is the part we run ourselves.

3. The parts and what each one does

The single source of truth is the anchor session's status. Pressing Open the room / Go live sets it to LIVE; Close the room sets it to COMPLETED (or back to SCHEDULED for a rehearsal before the start). Everything else, from the attendee page to the emails, reads that one field.

5. The rules each part follows

5.1 Cutting the video into pieces (MediaMTX)

  • Each piece is 4 seconds of video (hlsSegmentDuration). The playlist lists the newest 7 (hlsSegmentCount), about 28 seconds.
  • Why 4 and not 2: a piece can only be cut where Zoom sends a full frame (a keyframe), about every 3 seconds. At 2 seconds, on Nov 5, MediaMTX had to stretch the pieces mid-stream, and iPhones and iPads freeze on that change until the page is reopened. The piece length must stay at or above Zoom's keyframe gap.
  • Players start about 3 pieces behind the newest one, so the normal delay is about 12 seconds, plus Zoom's own delay.
  • Pieces are MPEG-TS (hlsVariant: mpegts), which every browser and the iPhone play. hlsAlwaysRemux means pieces are made even before anyone watches, so the first viewer does not wait.
  • When Zoom's push stops, MediaMTX removes the stream path at once. That is why a drop looks like "the stream is gone", not "the stream is slow".

5.2 Is the stream live? (stream-status)

  1. The player asks /api/public/events/{slug}/sessions/{id}/stream-status.
  2. The route asks MediaMTX for the playlist. A playlist means live. The answer is cached 3 seconds per container, so 5,000 viewers cause about one MediaMTX check every 3 seconds, not thousands.
  3. The stored status flips IDLE → ACTIVE when it appears and ACTIVE → ENDED when it disappears, and logs zoom:stream-status-changed. The flip happens only on a real change, so heavy polling cannot spam the database.
  4. It returns two addresses: the CDN one and the box ("origin") one.

5.3 The player (LivePlayer)

  • Start. Ask stream-status. Live: load the playlist (the browser's own HLS on Safari and Chrome, the hls.js library elsewhere). Not live: show the waiting card and ask again every 10 seconds.
  • Sound. Browsers only autoplay muted, so the video starts muted with a big "Tap to turn on sound" button. The choice is remembered per browser. If a reconnect is refused sound, the button comes back, so the viewer is never silently muted.
  • CDN failover. If the CDN copy fails, switch to the box copy, once per load (no ping-pong).
  • Recovery. If both fail, go back to asking stream-status every 10 seconds, and play again when it is live. No page reload is needed. A Retry button re-runs this without reloading the page, because 5,000 full reloads at once would overload the site.
  • Stall watchdog. The browser's own player often just freezes when the stream vanishes, with no error. So once a second the player checks whether the picture has moved:
    • It only starts watching after the picture has moved once (a slow first piece is not a drop).
    • Frozen for 15 to 20 s (random per viewer, so a crowd does not reconnect at the same moment): reconnect.
    • Each reconnect in a row doubles the wait: 15 s, 30 s, 60 s, then every 2 minutes. A minute of moving picture resets it.
    • A load that never shows a first frame is retried after 60 s, also backed off.
  • Back to live. A viewer who falls more than 24 s behind (Safari, Firefox) or is paused by the phone gets a "Back to live" button.
  • Wording. "Waiting for stream to start" before it has played; "The stream was interrupted, reconnecting" after a drop; past the scheduled end, "The stream has stopped, this page will show it in a few minutes".

5.4 Waiting room and admission

  • Each attendee page asks lobby-status every 15 to 20 seconds (random spread). The route keeps its answer for 3 seconds, so the database sees about one query per 3 seconds per container.
  • When the answer flips to "room open", the page fetches fresh join details once (a link fetched at page load may have expired during a long wait) and shows the player. A latch makes this happen once per opening; closing the room resets it.
  • The join route lets a registrant in while the session is LIVE, whatever the clock says, so a late joiner during an overrun still gets in.

5.5 Has the webinar ended?

One rule, webinarRoomEnded, used by the attendee page, the survey and the console:

if the room is open (LIVE)                    → not ended, however late
else if past the scheduled end                → ended
else if closed (COMPLETED) after the start    → ended   (an early close is the end)
else                                          → not ended (before start, or a rehearsal)

Once ended for a viewer, the page swaps the video for the thank-you screen, the survey pops up once (it comes back on the next visit until answered), and the exit button shows. Reopening the room brings everyone still on the page back.

5.6 Closing the room automatically (worker, every 3 minutes)

Ending in Zoom does not close our room by itself; the worker does, but carefully, because a host who crashes and restarts must not lose the audience.

  • Zoom says it ended. Close when Zoom's last end time is after the room opened, no earlier than 15 minutes before the scheduled end, and at least 5 minutes old (time for a host to restart).
  • Safety net. A room still open 2 hours after the scheduled end (or the last reopen) is closed as forgotten.
  • Overrun exception (custom stream only). Before the safety net closes a room, it checks the stream itself. If the room's own stream is still arriving, the webinar is still on air: keep it open. To avoid ending on a blip:
    • the stream must be missing for 5 minutes before it counts as gone (a Zoom reconnect takes about 40 s);
    • Zoom is still asked: a settled Zoom end closes the room even if something keeps streaming;
    • nothing stays open more than 6 hours after the scheduled end.

5.7 Restarting a dropped stream (worker, every minute)

Job webinar-stream-watchdog, at second 30 of every minute, for each open room in Custom stream mode:

  1. Ask MediaMTX whether the stream is arriving. Missing: look again 20 seconds later, so a quick reconnect is not a drop.
  2. Missing twice, after having been live since the room opened: a drop. It is logged as a warning (webinar-stream:dropped-while-live, visible on /logs) and put on the console's Stream timeline.
  3. Ask Zoom to start the push again, the same call as the Start stream button. At most 5 attempts per drop, waiting 1, 2, 4, 8 then 16 minutes between them. Zoom refuses when the host has ended the webinar, so a finished webinar is never restarted.
  4. When the stream is seen again: "Stream live, back after a drop" on the timeline, and the attempt count resets.

Details that keep it from doing harm:

  • Only a real "not there" from MediaMTX counts. A slow or unreachable MediaMTX is "no evidence", never a drop.
  • Just before asking Zoom, it reads the room again. If a producer pressed Stop or Start, closed or reopened the room, or changed the stream during the wait, it does nothing.
  • If Zoom answers that the webinar is not running (the host ended it), it stops trying for that drop at once.
  • The second look is 20 seconds after the first, inside the worker's shutdown time, so a deploy never leaves it switched off.

It leaves alone: a stream never live in this room opening, a stream the producer stopped with Stop stream (remembered until Start stream or the next room opening), Zoom embed mode, and closed rooms.

Stop the stream from the console, not from inside Zoom. The watchdog cannot tell a host pressing "Stop live stream" in Zoom's own client from a drop, so it would start the stream again within about a minute and a half.

5.8 The thank-you email

  • Scheduled for 30 minutes after the scheduled end.
  • While the room is open, the sender holds it and looks again every 10 minutes.
  • When the room closes (by the producer or the worker), it moves to 30 minutes after the close, earlier or later than planned.
  • The hold gives up 2.5 hours after the scheduled end, unless the custom stream is still on air (same 5-minute grace and 6-hour cap as above).

5.9 Q&A, polls and presence

  • Q&A and the poll come in one refresh every 15 seconds, so a poll adds no extra requests.
  • Only the open poll reaches attendees. Closing it takes it off their screen at once. Results show only to people who answered, and only if the producer switched them on. Tallies are cached 5 seconds.
  • Presence: each open page beats every 30 to 40 seconds (not while the tab is hidden). "Here now" means a beat in the last 60 seconds. This is our page's view, separate from Zoom's attendance report afterwards.

5.10 Who may push a stream

  • Each webinar has a random stream key. Zoom is given the key plus a password: an HMAC of the key signed with a server secret, so viewers, who see the key in the video address, can never publish.
  • MediaMTX can ask our app to approve every publish (/api/webhooks/mediamtx-auth). not switched on See Known gaps.

7. When the stream drops

Why did it drop?

Answered from MediaMTX's log: at 08:41:46 the RTMP connection from Zoom closed with EOF, a clean hang-up from Zoom's side, not a timeout on ours. Zoom did not reconnect by itself; the 08:42:38 connection (from a different Zoom server) is the producer's Start stream. Three minutes earlier, at 08:38:49, MediaMTX had warned segment duration changed from 2s to 3s - this will cause an error in iOS clients, which is the likelier cause of the iPhone viewers' freezes; the pieces are now 4 seconds (5.1).

In general, our app logs show that a stream stopped, not why. The app only sees the stream path disappear. The reason (Zoom closed the connection, a network timeout, a bad packet) is in MediaMTX's own log, which stays in Docker on the box and is not shipped to CloudWatch. It is kept for a limited time (log rotation), so read it soon:

docker logs --since 2026-11-05T08:35:00Z --until 2026-11-05T08:45:00Z ea-sys-mediamtx

Look for the RTMP connection closing just before 08:42:03, and the reason printed on that line. Typical causes: Zoom restarting its push (for example after a host changes the layout or the shared screen), the host's own network, or Zoom's live-stream service. If MediaMTX shows a clean close from Zoom's side, the cause is on Zoom's side.

What happens today when it drops

  • Attendees: the player shows "The stream was interrupted, reconnecting", checks every 10 seconds, and plays again by itself when the stream is back. The stall watchdog covers players that freeze without noticing.
  • Producers: on Nov 5 someone had to notice and press Start stream (35 seconds). Since then the stream watchdog (5.7) does it within about a minute, and the console's Stream timeline card shows the drop and the restart.

8. What the logs say, and where

Three places, from easiest to deepest:

  1. The console's Stream timeline card (Webinar Console, Setup tab, right column): room opened or closed, Start or Stop pressed and by whom, stream live, dropped, restart requested or refused. Refreshes every 15 s while the room is open. Kept with the event's audit log (action WEBINAR_STREAM).
  2. The app log: CloudWatch group ea-sys/app (and logs/app.log on the box). The lines below. The /logs page shows only warnings and errors, so there you see drops, refused restarts and an unreachable MediaMTX, not the routine starts.
  3. MediaMTX's own log: CloudWatch group ea-sys/mediamtx (and logs/mediamtx.log on the box), once the box steps in infra/cloudwatch/README.md are done. This is where the reason for a drop is: the RTMP connection opening, publishing, and closing with its cause.

9. Known gaps

  • Publish approval is not switched on. mediamtx.yml has no authMethod: http, so MediaMTX accepts a push on any path from anyone who can reach port 1935. The approval route and the publish passwords exist; switching it on is the owner step in docs/LIVE_STREAMING.md ("Publish authorisation").
  • MediaMTX's log reaches CloudWatch only after the box steps (install the logrotate rule, re-run the CloudWatch setup, recreate the MediaMTX container outside a webinar): see infra/cloudwatch/README.md, "MediaMTX log".
  • The watchdog cannot say why a stream dropped, only that it did; the reason is in MediaMTX's log.
  • Zoom embed mode cannot tell "still on air" from "left open", so a webinar running more than 2 hours past its scheduled end is closed by the safety net.
  • The MediaMTX image is bluenviron/mediamtx:latest, not a pinned version. Pin it: run docker exec ea-sys-mediamtx /mediamtx --version on the box and put that version in docker-compose.prod.yml, so a future MediaMTX that renames a config key cannot break the next restart.

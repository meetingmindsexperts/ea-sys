EA-SYS Platform · Architecture Decision Brief

How we keep each customer's data separate on the new platform

As we extend EA-SYS from Meeting Minds' own event system into a product other organizations can use, we must decide how their data is kept apart — from one another, and from Meeting Minds' own. This brief presents the two options and our recommendation.

Prepared for · MMG Leadership From · Development (Krishna) Date · August 2026 Status · Decision requested
● Recommendation

Adopt the two-silo model: keep Meeting Minds' event system on its own dedicated platform, and host external customers on a separate multi-tenant platform.

One product, two homes. It protects Meeting Minds' live data through physical separation, contains the impact of any incident, and lets us launch for the first customer without ever putting a live MMG conference at risk. We ask leadership to endorse this direction so the build proceeds on the safe path.

Context

Why this decision, and why now

EA-SYS today runs Meeting Minds' own conferences — registrations, speakers, schedules, finances. We are extending it into a platform that other organizations can use for their own events.

The moment we host another company's data, we take on a duty: their records must be invisible to every other customer, and Meeting Minds' own records must be held to the same standard. How we separate that data is a foundational choice — it shapes our security posture, our reliability, and our operating cost for years. There are two credible ways to do it.

The options

Two separate systems, or one shared system

Option ARecommended

Two separate systems (“two-silo”)

Meeting Minds' data stays on its own system, in its own database. External customers live on a new, separate platform. The same product, running in two isolated homes.

  • MMG data is physically apart from customers' — not just filtered
  • An incident on one system cannot touch the other
  • Meeting Minds keeps running exactly as it does today
  • More infrastructure to operate
Option BAlternative

One shared system (“single pool”)

Everyone — Meeting Minds and every customer — lives in one system and one database, with software rules keeping each organization's records apart.

  • One system, one plane over everything — simplest to operate
  • Separation depends entirely on a software “wall” we are still building
  • Requires converting Meeting Minds' live system to the shared model
  • A single fault can affect Meeting Minds and all customers together

What's actually at stake

The separation “wall,” in plain terms

In a shared system, the wall between customers is enforced by software — a strict filter that shows each organization only its own rows of data (in the industry this is called row-level access control). It is powerful, but it is only as strong as its coverage, and we are still extending that coverage across the system — roughly 14 of the data areas are done so far. Until it is complete and proven, any gap is a real exposure.

In two separate systems, Meeting Minds' protection does not depend on that wall at all. Its data simply isn't in the same database as any customer's. Even a total failure of the software wall on the customer platform leaves Meeting Minds untouched.

The support question

“If something breaks for a customer, can our team see in and fix it?”

Yes — under either model, and this was the question that prompted the review. Under the recommended two-silo model, our support team keeps full ability to diagnose:

Two capabilities, both preserved

System-wide monitoring — logs, health, and activity across the whole platform stay unified in one place. And step-into-a-customer support — an authorized super-admin can enter any customer's view to diagnose their issue, one customer at a time, fully controlled and audited. The only thing two-silo does not offer is a single login that also reaches into Meeting Minds' own records — and Meeting Minds we already operate directly. Operationally, we give up almost nothing.

Recommendation

Two-silo — and the reasoning in one paragraph

The operational convenience of a single shared system is not worth placing Meeting Minds' live production data in the same database as external customers while the separation wall is still being built. Two-silo delivers roughly 90% of that convenience — unified monitoring plus step-into-any-customer support — while keeping our most valuable data physically out of harm's way and every change's impact contained. The one convenience we forgo, we already have another way.

A middle path, kept open

Once the customer platform is proven, we can run a Meeting Minds event on it as a trial — gaining a presence there without moving our live production system. It gives us the best of both, deferred until the risk is low. The door to a unified model is not closed; it is simply not the bet to make now.

Risks & mitigations

Being honest about the cost of the recommendation

More infrastructure
Two systems cost more to run than one. Mitigation: the same software runs on both, deployed automatically — the added cost is incremental, not a second team.
Two support contexts
Meeting Minds and the platform are operated separately. Mitigation: unified monitoring plus the step-into-a-customer console keep day-to-day support simple.
Why not single-pool now
Its appeal is real, but it would put MMG's live data behind a wall that is ~14 areas complete, not finished — an exposure we should not accept today. Revisit when the wall is proven and the middle path has been trialed.

What we're asking for

The decision, and what follows it

  1. Endorse the two-silo topology as the direction for the platform.
  2. Then we stand up the customer platform as a fresh, isolated system — never touching MMG's production — and continue the security-hardening program already underway.
  3. We build the step-into-a-customer support console so our team can diagnose any customer issue safely and on demand.
  4. We revisit a unified model only if a compelling reason emerges, using the low-risk middle path (trialing an MMG event on the platform) as the bridge.

Glossary

Plain definitions for the terms above

Tenant
A single customer organization on the platform, with its own data and users. Keeping tenants separate is the whole challenge.
Two-silo
The recommendation: two separate systems — one for Meeting Minds, one for customers — each with its own database.
Single pool
The alternative: one shared system and database for everyone, with software keeping records apart.
Row-level access control
The software “wall” in a shared system — a database-enforced filter so each organization sees only its own rows. Strong, but only as good as its coverage.
Blast radius
How far the damage spreads when something goes wrong. Two-silo contains it; single-pool widens it.
Dogfooding
Running our own event on the customer platform to prove it before customers rely on it — the middle path.

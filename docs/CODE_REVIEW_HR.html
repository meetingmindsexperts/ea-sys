Adversarial Code Review: HR Module

Attendance and UAE leave tracking (/hr, /hr/employees, /hr/attendance, /api/hr/*, src/hr/**). Reviewed at main = 9de399c3 on Aug 31, 2026, which includes the same-day rulings 644bd60a (annual leave charged across a block) and 9de399c3 (agreed entitlement override). Three independent reviewer passes (security/tenancy/privacy, correctness/maths/concurrency, UI/drift/tests) plus a consolidating pass that re-verified every finding against source; the two headline balance defects were reproduced by running computeLeaveBalance on the current tree. Status after batch 1 (same day). FIXED all six HIGHs in code, each with pinning tests; the guards for H1 to H5 were mutation-verified (reverting the guard fails exactly that test). H3 was fixed after an owner ruling of "calendar days everywhere"; H6 after an owner decision to store each employee's seed year on the row. Of the MEDs, M9 was fixed the same day once the owner ruled ("the shutdown wins"); nothing else below H6 has been touched, and the deferred list lives in docs/ROADMAP.md under "HR module review".

The short version. The foundations are sound: the role predicate is narrow and fails closed, API keys cannot reach HR at all, every route runs auth then denyNonHr then requireOrgId then runWithTenant, every read and write is org-bound, six RLS policies plus a harness cover the platform case, audit rows are stamped from the lane, and no non-HR role can see an HR row through any surface including the audit feed. The real defects cluster in three places: (1) the balance engine fails open on two edges (a year with no employment window, and the first-year gate judged at today rather than at exit), both reachable from the "Show leavers" toggle and both producing a plausible-looking wrong payroll figure; (2) two recording paths for the same annual leave charge different amounts, and four documents disagree about which is right; (3) attendance history is not recoverable once a drag lands wrong, because the grid's keyboard handler fires on Cmd/Ctrl combinations, clears are unbounded hard deletes, and the attendance audit stores no before-state.

HIGH H1. A year the person was not employed in gets a full balance with taken summed over their entire history

src/hr/lib/leave-balance.ts (window → from/to null) · src/hr/lib/hr-leave-year.ts:100-109 · src/hr/lib/hr-date.ts:95-103 · reachable from src/app/(dashboard)/hr/page.tsx "Show leavers" → getOrgLeaveSummary (leave-balance-service.ts:181 filters on status only)

employedWindowInYear correctly returns null when the employment window and the leave year do not overlap. computeLeaveBalance then does from = window?.from ?? null; to = window?.to ?? null, and sumWeights calls isWithin(date, null, null), which is true for every entry. So "not employed this year" degrades to "no bound at all". Entitlement is still judged at asOf (30), carried-in is still the seed.

// reproduced on HEAD with npx tsx
// leaver: joined 2020-01-15, exit 2025-06-30, 2 AL in 2024, 3 AL in 2025, 1 SL-F in 2025, seed carryover 4
window 2026: null
2026 summary row -> { entitlement: 30, carriedIn: 4, taken: 5, balance: 29, sickFullUsed: 1 }
// a 2027 joiner with one 2027 AL entry, viewed in 2026
2026 row -> { taken: 1, balance: -1 }

Failure scenario. Today: any employee record with an exit date before 2026-01-01 shows a fabricated 2026 row under "Show leavers". From 1 January 2027: every 2026 leaver (there are several in the live data) shows Entitled 30, taken = their whole 2026 leave, balance = 30 minus that, for a year they were not employed for a single day. That is the figure somebody will read off during a final settlement query. hr-date.test.ts pins that the window is null; nothing pins what the engine does with null.

Fix. When the window is null, return a zeroed annual/sick block with an explicit employedInYear: false flag and have the summary render "not employed this year" rather than numbers. Pin with a test that feeds a pre-year leaver into computeLeaveBalance and asserts taken === 0.

FIXED batch 1: computeLeaveBalance now feeds an empty entry list, zero entitlement and zero carry-in through when the window is null and returns employedInYear: false; the summary renders "Not employed in {year} (left ...)" across the numeric columns and excludes such rows from the two warning counts. Four tests, three of which fail when the guard is reverted.

What to learn. A guard that turns "no window" into "no bound" is the fail-open shape. null here means "nothing to count", and the safe translation of that is zero, never "everything". The same predicate (isWithin treating a null bound as open) is correct for the rule window and wrong for the year window; the caller has to decide, not the helper.

HIGH H2. The first-year entitlement gate is judged at today's date, not at the exit date, so a leaver who never completed twelve months is handed 30 days once the calendar passes the anniversary they never reached

src/hr/lib/leave-balance.ts (hasCompletedFirstYear(employee.joiningDate, asOf)) · src/hr/lib/hr-leave-year.ts:64-69

Neither function looks at exitDate. The window bounds the taken sum but not the gate.

// reproduced on HEAD: joined 2025-09-15, last working day 2026-08-15 (11 months), one AL day in May
asOf 2026-08-31 -> { firstYear: false, entitlement: 0,  balance: -1 }
asOf 2026-09-15 -> { firstYear: true,  entitlement: 30, balance: 29 }   // the anniversary she never reached
asOf 2026-10-01 -> { firstYear: true,  entitlement: 30, balance: 29 }

Failure scenario. The owner's Aug 31 ruling is "zero, unless first year is completed; leaver or not". The automatic figure contradicts it for exactly the population the ruling was about: on the day after their would-be anniversary, every leaver who left inside their first year flips from 0 to 30 on the leavers view, with no marker. The new annualEntitlementDays override lets HR type the agreed number over it, but the default is wrong and looks like a rule.

Fix. One line: evaluate the gate at min(asOf, exitDate ?? asOf). Pin "exit before anniversary, viewed after it" → entitlement 0.

FIXED batch 1: the gate is judged at the earlier of asOf and the exit date. Three tests (stays at zero after the would-be anniversary; a leaver who did complete the year keeps 30; an active employee is unchanged); reverting the gate date fails the first.

What to learn. A balance "as at" a date has two clocks: the calendar and the employment. Anything that says "has this person reached X" must be evaluated at whichever of the two stopped first.

HIGH H3. The same annual leave costs 12 days recorded from the grid and 10 days recorded as a Company day, the grid also charges public holidays inside the block, and four documents disagree about which is right

src/hr/services/attendance-service.ts:133-151 · src/hr/lib/hr-constants.ts:65-67 · src/hr/lib/hr-effective-status.ts:79-84 · tests __tests__/hr/hr-attendance-rules.test.ts:262-268 and :389-403

  • Explicit range (grid, API, importer): rangeCoversCalendarDays("ANNUAL") is true, so the holiday lookup is skipped entirely and every calendar day in the block is written as AL, weekends and public holidays. The five-day Eid al-Adha block (25 to 29 May, all seeded PH) selected as AL costs 5 AL days for a week nobody worked.
  • ORG rule carrying AL (Company day dialog; any code is selectable): effectiveStatusFor resolves PH and OFF before the rule, so ruleDerivedDays charges working days only. Same shutdown, 10 days.

Both behaviours are pinned by tests that pass together. And the written record contradicts itself: the attendance-service.ts header (lines 10-13) says "A BULK RANGE EXPANDS TO WORKING DAYS ONLY ... not twelve calendar days" (stale since 644bd60a, which edited the same file and left the header); hr-constants.ts records the ruling for twelve; the root CLAUDE.md HR entry still calls the fork "OPEN" and says "the app's recording path explicitly refuses to"; docs/HR_MODULE_PLAN.md §6 still says "expands to working days only, skipping weekends and holidays"; the Company day dialog copy (attendance/page.tsx:782-785) promises weekends and holidays are left alone, which is true for rules and false for the grid. The user guide was updated and is the only document that matches the code.

Failure scenario. A two-week office shutdown recorded by dragging the grid charges 23 people 12 days each; the same shutdown recorded as a Company day charges 10 each. 46 days of annual leave hinge on which button was pressed, and a reader "fixing" the service to match its own header reverses a payroll ruling.

Fix. Owner call first, on two questions the Aug 31 ruling did not cover: is a public holiday inside an AL block charged, and does a rule-recorded AL block follow the same rule as an explicit one? Then make ruleDerivedDays honour rangeCoversCalendarDays(rule.category) (or refuse leave-carrying codes on ORG rules), and rewrite the header, the CLAUDE.md paragraph, plan §6 and the dialog copy in the same commit.

FIXED batch 1, after an owner ruling of "calendar days everywhere": AttendanceRuleLike carries the code's category, and a rule whose category covers calendar days now takes precedence over the holiday and the weekend in effectiveStatusFor (a working-day rule such as WFH still falls through to PH/OFF). An AL shutdown therefore costs 12 as a rule and 12 as a range, a public holiday inside an AL block is charged on both paths, and a rule with no category fails safe to working days. The service header, CLAUDE.md, plan §5.6/§6/§17.3, the Company-day and Standing dialog copy and the user guide all describe the one rule. Tests: the weekend-spanning AL rule now pins 12 (with a WFH sibling pinning 10), plus the Saturday, holiday and no-category cases on the resolver.

What to learn. This module's own rule is that one fact must have one implementation. Rules and entries are two recording paths for the same fact, and the policy function exists, but only one path calls it. When a policy changes, grep for every place that answers the same question before closing the ticket.

HIGH H4. The grid's keyboard shortcuts ignore modifier keys, key repeat and the busy flag: Cmd+C writes comp-off across the selection, Cmd+A writes annual leave, Cmd+S sick leave, Cmd+W work-from-home while the tab closes

src/app/(dashboard)/hr/attendance/page.tsx:255-268

const code = KEY_TO_CODE[ev.key.toLowerCase()];      // ev.key is still "c" with Cmd held
if (code) { ev.preventDefault(); void apply(code); return; }

Failure scenario. HR drags across a week to copy the pattern somewhere (the spreadsheet reflex is Cmd+C) and five CO rows are written; the toast reads as success. Cmd+A writes AL and swallows select-all; Cmd+S writes SL-F and swallows the save dialog; Cmd+O writes OD. Browsers do not honour preventDefault on Cmd/Ctrl+W, so the tab closes with the WFH PUT in flight and nobody sees a toast. Holding a auto-repeats keydown and fires apply() on every repeat before the first resolves (the selection is only cleared at the end), issuing dozens of PUTs into the 300/hr write bucket. Pressing a then s quickly races two loops over the same cells. Combined with H5, none of it can be reconstructed.

Fix. First line of onKey: if (ev.metaKey || ev.ctrlKey || ev.altKey || ev.repeat || busy) return; and add busy to the effect deps.

FIXED batch 1: modifier and repeat keys are refused before the lookup, and a synchronous applying ref makes apply() single-flight (a second keypress or a popover double-click during a write is ignored). Source-pinned in hr-attendance-rules.test.ts; deleting the guard line fails the pin.

What to learn. A document-level key handler shares the keyboard with the browser. Anything that maps single letters must filter modifiers and repeats first, or it will eventually write data when the user meant to copy it.

HIGH H5. Attendance history is unrecoverable: clearing is an unbounded, unthrottled hard delete audited by count, and an overwrite records only the new code and wipes the remark

src/app/api/hr/attendance/route.ts:175-215 (DELETE: no rate limit) · src/hr/services/attendance-service.ts:227-265 (clearAttendance: no MAX_RANGE_DAYS, deleteMany, audit { removed: count }) · :162-211 (upsert audit stores { code, from, to, days }, no before-state) · :182-187 (update sets remarks: input.remarks?.trim() || null)

  • The PUT path has a 366-day cap and a 300/hr limit; the DELETE path has neither. DELETE /api/hr/attendance { employeeId, from: "2000-01-01", to: "2100-12-31" } erases one person's entire recorded history in one call; ~23 calls erase the org's. The audit says "removed: 41" per person and nothing else. Recovery is the hourly DR dump, which may already contain the deletion.
  • A drag that lands AL over a recorded SL-F week converts sick leave into annual leave, moving both the annual balance and the sick tier, and the trail says only "AL 2026-03-02..06, 5 days". The Employee audit stores before/after; the attendance audit does not.
  • The grid never sends remarks, so every keypress on an existing entry nulls its remark ("medical certificate on file" is gone). No UI shows remarks, so the loss is invisible.

Fix. Apply MAX_RANGE_DAYS and the hr-write limit to DELETE; inside the transaction read the affected rows first and store { date, oldCode, newCode }[] (and the deleted { date, code }[]) in changes; only touch remarks when the caller supplied it.

FIXED batch 1: clearAttendance has the same 366-day ceiling as a write and runs read-then-delete in one transaction, storing every removed { date, code } on the audit row; setAttendance reads the affected days inside its transaction and stores overwritten: [{ date, previousCode }]; remarks is written only when the caller supplied the key (an explicit null still clears); the DELETE route shares the hr-write rate bucket and its rejection log carries the employee and user. Seven tests in the new hr-attendance-service.test.ts; the range cap and the remarks guard are each mutation-verified.

What to learn. An audit row that stores only the after-state cannot reverse anything. The test is "could I put it back from the audit alone?" Employee edits pass that test; attendance edits do not.

HIGH H6. 1 January 2027 is a cliff: no year-end roll exists, LeaveGrant is never read or written, and the go-live seeds are re-applied to every year

src/hr/services/leave-balance-service.ts (both callers omit carriedInDays) · src/hr/lib/leave-balance.ts (carriedInDays ?? employee.carryoverDays; openingSickUsed added for whatever leaveYear is asked) · planYearRoll has no caller · grep: zero references to leaveGrant outside the schema, the RLS policy and the harness cleanup

Failure scenario. First working day of 2027, no operator action: an employee who closed 2026 at -15 shows a 2027 balance of 30, not 15; every 2026 overdraft or surplus vanishes; anyone seeded with openingSickUsed = 10 starts 2027 already 10 into the full-pay tier. FIXED batch 1, after an owner decision to store the seed year on the row: new nullable Employee.seedLeaveYear (additive migration, backfilled from createdAt; the import writes 2026; the service stamps the current year), and the engine applies the seeds in that year only. leave-year-roll-service.ts writes one LeaveGrant per employee still employed on 1 January (carry-in from planYearRoll, capped one way; one audit row per run with the capped set); both balance paths read carriedInDays from the grant. The hr-year-roll worker job (lease 1018, 01:05 UTC daily) runs the roll for every org in January, nightly, because it is an idempotent upsert that recomputes from the rows; POST /api/hr/leave-year/roll and a "Re-run carry-over" button on the summary are the manual re-run. MED M1. A leaver vanishes from the attendance grid the moment the exit is recorded, so notice-period leave cannot be entered and past months cannot be corrected

attendance/page.tsx:112 (useHrEmployees(), default includeExited=false) · src/app/api/hr/employees/route.ts:84 (status filter) · employee-service.ts (exit sets status immediately, regardless of a future date)

Record a leaver with last working day 30 September. The row disappears from /hr/attendance for September and every past month. The API accepts the writes (it checks only the employment window); the UI cannot reach them, and there is no other surface for a leaver's history. The "records" tile still counts their entries, so the two tiles disagree by construction. Fix: useHrEmployees(true) on the grid and filter rows to those whose employment overlaps the visible month.

MED M2. status and exitDate are independently editable, and the two halves of the module read different fields

src/app/api/hr/employees/[employeeId]/route.ts:15-28 · src/app/api/hr/employees/route.ts:47 (create accepts status; createEmployee silently drops it) · employee-service.ts (validates only exit < joining)

Lists and the summary filter on status; every window check reads exitDate. PATCH { status: "RESIGNED" } with no date hides the person from the default views while the balance engine and the write path treat them as employed forever. The reverse (a past exit date, status still ACTIVE) keeps a leaver in the active payroll table. The edit form sends both fields on every save, so clearing the date and forgetting the dropdown produces the first state. Fix: enforce the pair in updateEmployee (non-ACTIVE requires an exit date; clearing the date restores ACTIVE, or reject) and drop status from the create schema.

MED M3. Moving the joining date later or the exit date earlier silently strands recorded leave: hidden in the grid, excluded from the balance, rows still in the table

employee-service.ts updateEmployee (never counts entries outside the resulting window) · hr-effective-status.ts:74-78 (NOT_EMPLOYED beats an explicit entry) · attendance/page.tsx:446, 470 (rendered opacity-0, unselectable)

The write path refuses out-of-window rows precisely so "the data cannot contain a leave day for somebody who did not work here"; the edit path lets the window move under existing rows. Setting an exit to 31 October by mistake for someone with AL recorded through 20 November drops their taken figure, flips a negative balance positive, and blanks the November cells with no warning. Fix: count entries outside the resulting window and refuse (ENTRIES_OUTSIDE_WINDOW, naming the count) or require an explicit force recorded in the audit.

MED M4. ?year= on /api/hr/summary and /api/hr/balances/[id] returns numbers labelled with a year they were not computed for

leave-balance-service.ts (asOf is always today) · summary/route.ts:29-34, balances/[employeeId]/route.ts:25-30 (accept 2000-2100)

For a prior year the first-year gate is judged today, carried-in is the 2026 seed, openingSickUsed is re-added, nextAnniversary is relative to today, and H1 applies to anyone who joined after that year. The UI never sends year, so this is API-only today, but the routes validate and accept it as if it worked. Fix: clamp asOf to the end of the requested year and refuse years with no LeaveGrant until H6 ships.

MED M5. POST /api/hr/employees links any userId with no org or existence check, it cannot be unlinked, and nothing reads it

src/app/api/hr/employees/route.ts:45 (userId: z.string().cuid().nullish()) · employee-service.ts (userId: input.userId ?? null, no lookup) · Employee.userId is a global @unique FK

On the platform, org B can bind one of its employees to an org A login; org A then gets USER_ALREADY_LINKED (409) for its own employee, a cross-tenant squat and an existence oracle for user ids. On master an employee can be bound to a REGISTRANT or REVIEWER account. A non-existent id is a P2003 that falls through to UNKNOWN (500 plus an error-level log that pages). patchSchema omits userId, so a wrong link is permanent through the API, and the schema comment ("used from day one ... self-entries") is not true: no code reads the column. Fix: db.user.findFirst({ where: { id, organizationId } }) in createEmployee, refuse otherwise; allow userId: null on PATCH.

MED M6. updateEmployee writes by bare id while its own comment claims the org binding is "part of the write"

employee-service.ts: org-bound findFirst, then db.employee.update({ where: { id: input.employeeId } }); setEmployeeExit inherits it. Compare deleteAttendanceRule, which does it correctly with deleteMany({ where: { id, organizationId } }).

Not exploitable today (ids are immutable, the read gates, RLS would filter on the platform), but it is exactly the "org check in a preceding read that a refactor can drop" shape the codebase forbids, and tests/tenancy/hr-rls.test.ts has no defence-#1-in-isolation assertion for it where other domains do. Fix: updateMany({ where: { id, organizationId } }) and re-read, plus the harness assertion.

MED M7. Employee edits copy the full record, including free-text notes, into AuditLog twice, forever; rule creation audits the free-text label; rule deletion audits nothing about the rule

employee-service.ts (before: toEmployeeView(existing), after: toEmployeeView(row); EMPLOYEE_SELECT includes notes) · attendance-rule-service.ts (create audits label; delete audits { source: "ui" })

The attendance audit explicitly refuses to store remarks because free text will hold medical detail and the trail outlives the entry. MED M12. A full-year range runs up to 366 sequential upserts inside one interactive transaction with Prisma's default 5-second timeout

attendance-service.ts:163-190 (tenantTransaction with no opts) · src/lib/db.ts:497-508 (passes through; Prisma 6.19 defaults: maxWait 2 s, timeout 5 s)

Lessons

  • A null bound is not a zero bound. The engine's only two wrong figures come from null meaning "open" in one helper and "nothing" in the caller's intent. Name the case (employedInYear: false) instead of letting it fall through.
  • "As at" has two clocks. Anything gated on elapsed service must be judged at the earlier of today and the exit date.
  • One fact, one implementation, including the second path. The policy function existed; the rule path never called it. A policy change is not done until every path that answers the question has been grepped.
  • An audit you cannot restore from is a log, not an audit. Store the before-state on overwrite and delete, and cap and throttle the destructive verb the same as the constructive one.
  • Comments are load-bearing here. Four documents describe the annual-leave rule and three are wrong after a same-day ruling. The header of the file that implements it is the one a future engineer will trust.
